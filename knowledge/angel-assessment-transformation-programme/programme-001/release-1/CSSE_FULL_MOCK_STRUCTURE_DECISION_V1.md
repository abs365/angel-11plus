## 5. Question Count Decision: **B — Evidence-Supported Range**

**Not a single exact count.** The real examination itself does not use one — Comprehension varies 12-16 questions, Mathematics varies 20-21, across the only three years CSSE has published. Presenting a single fixed number as "the" CSSE structure would misrepresent evidence that itself shows legitimate variation. What **is** stable, and should anchor Angel's mock, is the **mark and timing structure** — 60/60 marks, the section timings, the component ordering — not a fixed item count.

**Recommended ranges:**
- English Comprehension: **12-16 questions**, ~35-40 marks
- Applied Reasoning: **5 questions**, 5 marks (structurally stable — but see §7, currently blocked)
- Mathematics: **20-21 questions**, 60 marks
- Continuous Writing: **2 prompts**, marks disclosed as Angel-internal (§7)

## 7. Continuous Writing Control — Respected Explicitly

Per the Evidence Review's own finding, restated and enforced here: **no numeric Continuous Writing score is proposed as CSSE-equivalent.** What can be implemented now: the authentic 2-prompt task structure, the reflective/narrative genre split, the ~20-minute timing, and "at least six sentences" instruction — all STABLE, well-evidenced. What remains blocked: any claim that a resulting score maps to real CSSE marks or bands. If Continuous Writing is included in an Angel Full Mock before that gate lifts, it must be presented as an authentic-task, Angel-internal-feedback component — not a marked, CSSE-equivalent section contributing to a combined score in the way Comprehension/AR/Maths can.

## 8. Five Mock/Practice Types — Purpose and Boundaries

| Type | Purpose | Must not be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Full CSSE Mock** | Reproduce the whole sitting's workload, pacing, and section transitions — the closest rehearsal of exam day as a single experience | Not a diagnostic tool; not for isolating one weak area |
| **2. English Subject Mock** | Comprehension + AR + CW together, English's own internal 60-mark/three-section structure, without the Maths paper | Not "half the Full Mock" in difficulty terms — it's the real, complete English paper structure on its own |
| **3. Mathematics Subject Mock** | The real 60-mark/60-minute Maths paper alone | Distinct timing pressure from the Full Mock (no fatigue from a prior English sitting) |
| **4. Continuous Writing Subject Mock** | The real 2-prompt, ~20-minute task structure alone, for genre/timing practice | Not a scored assessment until §7's gate lifts — purely task-authentic practice |
| **5. Focused competency practice** | Short, targeted sessions on one Question Type or competency (the existing `generatePersonalisedSession()` mechanism) | Not exam-paced, not exam-structured — deliberately smaller and untimed-or-lightly-timed, the opposite design goal to the four mock types above |

Each serves a genuinely different educational purpose (workload rehearsal vs. subject-specific pacing vs. task-genre familiarity vs. skill-building) — none is a scaled-down or scaled-up version of another, and none should be presented to a family as interchangeable.

## Answer

**"How many questions should Angel set for one full authentic CSSE mock?"**

**Not a single fixed number — an evidence-supported range: 12-16 English Comprehension questions, 5 Applied Reasoning questions (content currently blocked), and 20-21 Mathematics questions, totalling roughly 37-42 scored items, plus 2 Continuous Writing prompts sat as task-authentic but not-yet-numerically-scored. The one truly fixed, stable number is marks, not questions: 60 English + 60 Mathematics = 120 total, identical across all three years CSSE has published — that is the figure Angel's mock structure should be built to reproduce with confidence, while the item counts within each section are deliberately allowed to vary between individual mock papers, exactly as the real examination's own three years show.**
