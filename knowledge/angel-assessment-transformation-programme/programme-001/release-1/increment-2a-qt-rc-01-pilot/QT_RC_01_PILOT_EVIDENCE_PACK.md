## 2. Observed Task Structure — OFFICIAL FACT

Each question names a specific character and scene/moment ("during the midday dinner," "for her supper") and asks for one explicitly-stated fact. The stem's own wording narrows the search space to a specific passage region — a candidate does not need to search the whole text.

## 3. Assessed Skill / Competency — MIXTURE OF OFFICIAL FACT AND FRAMEWORK MAPPING

The task itself (locate and reproduce a stated fact) is directly observed (OFFICIAL FACT). Its mapping to "RC-01 (Literal Retrieval from Narrative Text)" is Assessment Brain V1's own competency label — CSSE does not publish competency names for its own questions (EDUCATIONAL INTERPRETATION / FRAMEWORK MAPPING, not a CSSE-stated fact).

## 4. Reasoning Requirement — EDUCATIONAL INTERPRETATION

One step: locate the stated fact, transcribe or lightly paraphrase it. No inference, no judgement, no synthesis across multiple passage locations. This characterisation is this pilot's own analysis of the observed pattern, not a CSSE-published claim.

## 5. Response Format — OFFICIAL FACT

Short open-ruled answer, 1-3 lines of blank space provided in the source paper. No tick-box, no option list.

## 6. Wording Characteristics — OFFICIAL FACT (directly observed)

Stems are short (typically one sentence), name the character and scene explicitly, and use plain interrogative form ("What... did...?", "How did... feel...?"). No embedded sub-clauses or compound conditions.

## 7. Information Density — EDUCATIONAL INTERPRETATION

Low: one fact requested per question, one location in the passage sufficient to answer.

## 8. Reading Burden — EDUCATIONAL INTERPRETATION

Low: answer is typically locatable within a narrow, question-cued span of the passage (2-4 lines), not requiring a full-passage re-read.

## 9. Mark Structure — OFFICIAL FACT

1 mark per instance, consistently, across all four directly-observed CSSE-008 examples and (per AEP-004's citation, not independently re-verified this pilot) across all three years.
