## Family: `wave2-fam-multiselect` (RC-01/RC-02, new)

**A. What the child needs to know:** a multi-select question has a specific number of correct answers, disclosed in the question. Selecting more than that number, even if some extra selections are correct, loses all the marks for the question. This is a real, directly-evidenced CSSE rule (the 2023 paper's own cover-page instruction: "Candidates must NOT tick more boxes than they are instructed to. Any who do will lose all the marks for that question"), not an Angel invention.
**B. How Angel explains it:** "Check each option against the passage one at a time. Only tick exactly the number you're told to tick, even if you think other options might also be true."
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** work through each lettered option methodically, asking "does the passage directly say or clearly show this?" before ticking, rather than scanning for a general impression of plausibility.
**D. What the child should notice:** distractor options in this format are often true-sounding statements that describe something that DIDN'T happen, or something from a different part of the passage than the one being asked about.
**E. Common traps:** over-selection (ticking a 5th option "just in case", losing all marks); under-selection (only finding 2 of 4, running out of confidence); selecting an option that is true of the passage generally but not one of the specific things asked about.
**F. Efficient exam method:** go through the options in order, A to H, checking each one individually rather than jumping between them, and count your ticks before moving on to make sure you have exactly the right number.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel could show 2 options already correctly checked and ask the learner to find the remaining ones — not yet built as a distinct guided mode this wave (see 007C report), recorded as Wave 3 scope.
**H. Independent practice:** full multi-select, unaided.
**I. Transfer practice:** a multi-select question on an unfamiliar passage's sequence of actions, not a repeat of a drilled option set.
**J. After a wrong answer:** Angel distinguishes over-selection ("you ticked more than asked, which loses all marks even though some were right") from under-selection ("you found some but not all of the correct options") — a real, evidence-grounded distinction, not a generic "incorrect."
**K. After repeated success:** increase the number of plausible distractors relative to correct options, making the recognition task genuinely harder rather than just longer.

## Cross-family principle (unchanged from Wave 1)

No internal terminology (family_id, QT codes, tier names) is ever shown to the learner. The over-selection rule is taught as a real exam-strategy fact, not softened or hidden, since it directly affects how a child should approach the real exam.
