# English Wave 1 Teaching Cards V1

**Prepared:** Educational Increment 007B, 2026-08-13.
**Covers:** the 8 question families authored in this wave (`scripts/generate-english-wave1.mjs`), each grounded in a pattern directly read from the 2022/2023 CSSE Main Test papers and marking scheme (007A evidence, CSSE-003/005/008).
**Format:** short teaching cards, not full lessons — per the 007B directive, "this does not mean every question needs a long lesson... use the appropriate intervention." Each card follows the required A-K structure at the granularity a family, not an individual question, warrants.

---

## Family: `wave1-fam-direct-retrieval` (RC-01)

**A. What the child needs to know:** the answer to a retrieval question is always a fact the passage states directly — it does not need to be worked out or inferred.
**B. How Angel explains it:** "This question is asking you to find something the passage already tells you. You don't need to guess or explain — just find it and write it down."
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** show a worked example: read the question, identify the key word(s) in it, scan the passage for that word or a close match, quote or paraphrase the sentence containing it.
**D. What the child should notice:** the question's own wording often echoes the passage's wording closely — that overlap is the fastest way in.
**E. Common traps:** answering with a fact from elsewhere in the passage that sounds similar but doesn't answer what was actually asked; adding inference ("because he was scared") when the question only wants the stated fact.
**F. Efficient exam method:** underline the key noun/verb in the question, scan for it in the passage, confirm the surrounding sentence answers the question before writing.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel highlights the relevant sentence for the first attempt in a family, and asks the child to identify which part of it answers the question.
**H. Independent practice:** no highlighting; the child locates the evidence unaided.
**I. Transfer practice:** a retrieval question on an unfamiliar passage the child has not seen guided support on.
**J. After a wrong answer:** show the correct sentence in the passage and ask the child to explain, in their own words, why it answers the question — turning the correction into a retrieval-location exercise, not just an answer reveal.
**K. After repeated success:** reduce scaffolding faster than other families — retrieval is the foundation skill, and early automaticity here frees attention for the harder families.

## Family: `wave1-fam-vocab-explain` / `wave1-fam-synonym-battery` (RC-03)

**A. What the child needs to know:** a word's meaning in a specific sentence can differ from its most common dictionary meaning — the surrounding words are the real evidence.
**B. How Angel explains it:** "Cover the tricky word and read the sentence around it. What kind of word is missing — a feeling, an action, a description? That's your clue."
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** demonstrate substitution — try a guessed synonym in place of the word and check the sentence still makes sense.
**D. What the child should notice:** tone words around the target word (is the surrounding sentence positive, negative, urgent, calm?) usually rule out half of the plausible guesses immediately.
**E. Common traps:** using the word's most common meaning rather than its meaning in this context (007A evidence: 2023's "bask" rejects "bathe" even though bathing is happening in the scene); confusing a word with a similar-looking but unrelated one (2022's "mournful"/"morning").
**F. Efficient exam method:** locate the exact line reference given in the question first — do not rely on memory of the whole passage.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel shows two plausible-looking wrong meanings alongside the right one and asks the child to explain, using the surrounding sentence, why each wrong one doesn't fit.
**H. Independent practice:** open explanation, no options shown.
**I. Transfer practice:** the same underlying skill (meaning-from-context) applied to a genuinely new word in a new passage, not a repeat of a drilled word.
**J. After a wrong answer:** point back to the exact sentence, not the word in isolation, and ask what clue in that sentence rules out the child's answer.
**K. After repeated success:** introduce words with more than one common meaning, so context-dependence itself becomes the skill being tested, not just unfamiliar vocabulary.

## Family: `wave1-fam-tick-justify` (RC-02)

**A. What the child needs to know:** the tick (Yes/No) is worth little on its own — the real skill, and the real marks, are in selecting reasons that are actually evidence for that specific tick.
**B. How Angel explains it:** "Imagine you have to convince someone who disagrees with you. What in the passage would you point to?"
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** walk through discarding a plausible-sounding reason that doesn't actually support the ticked answer, showing that not every true statement is relevant evidence.
**D. What the child should notice:** CSSE evidence (007A) shows credit is often given even when the tick itself is wrong, if the reasoning is sound and evidenced — reasoning quality matters independently of the tick.
**E. Common traps:** picking a reason that is true of the passage but doesn't actually justify the specific tick chosen; confusing what a character does with what a character feels.
**F. Efficient exam method:** decide the tick first from a quick overall impression, then go back to the passage specifically hunting for evidence for that side, not the other.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel provides the tick and asks the child to find the supporting evidence only.
**H. Independent practice:** full tick-plus-justify, unaided.
**I. Transfer practice:** the same reasoning structure applied to a character/scenario the child has not seen a worked example of.
**J. After a wrong answer:** if the tick was wrong but reasoning was evidenced, credit the reasoning explicitly and explain only why the tick itself doesn't hold up — do not treat the whole answer as a failure.
**K. After repeated success:** remove the tick-box scaffold entirely and ask for a free-standing justified judgement.

## Family: `wave1-fam-quote-explain` (RC-02, the heaviest-weighted CSSE pattern)

**A. What the child needs to know:** a quotation on its own earns nothing — it must be paired with an explanation of what it shows, and the explanation must connect specifically to the quotation chosen, not restate the question.
**B. How Angel explains it:** "First find the exact words. Then explain, in your own words, what those exact words tell us."
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** demonstrate the two-step structure explicitly: [quotation] → [what this literally says] → [what it shows/implies about the question asked].
**D. What the child should notice:** the strongest quotations are often short and specific, not long passages copied wholesale.
**E. Common traps:** paraphrasing instead of quoting exactly (007A evidence: CSSE marking explicitly rejects paraphrase here even when it's accurate); an explanation that just repeats the quotation in different words without adding insight.
**F. Efficient exam method:** find the quotation first, then ask "so what?" out loud before writing the explanation.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel supplies the quotation and asks only for the explanation.
**H. Independent practice:** both quotation-selection and explanation, unaided.
**I. Transfer practice:** the same two-step structure applied to inferring a different quality (motive, character trait, physical state) than previously practised.
**J. After a wrong answer:** if the quotation is right but the explanation is weak, show what a stronger explanation adds (the "so what") rather than just marking it wrong outright.
**K. After repeated success:** ask for two independent quotation+explanation pairs supporting the same judgement, matching CSSE's own multi-quotation questions.

## Family: `wave1-fam-sequencing` (RC-04)

**A. What the child needs to know:** sequencing marks are earned per item AND per position — being right about what happened is not the same as being right about the order (007A evidence: CSSE's own marking gives partial credit exactly this way).
**B. How Angel explains it:** "List everything you remember first, in any order. Then go back and number them using the passage, not your memory."
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** show the risk of relying on memory alone versus re-checking each item's position against the actual text.
**D. What the child should notice:** time-markers in the passage (then, next, after, later, two hours later) are direct signals for sequencing questions.
**E. Common traps:** getting all items right but in the wrong order (rewarded far less than the CSSE evidence's own precedent suggests learners assume); including something true of the passage that isn't actually one of the events asked for.
**F. Efficient exam method:** re-scan the passage in reading order once, ticking off each requested item as encountered, rather than trying to recall the order from memory.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel supplies the correct items unordered and asks the child to order them only.
**H. Independent practice:** both identification and ordering, unaided.
**I. Transfer practice:** sequencing in a passage with a less linear structure (e.g. a flashback or an epistolary passage reflecting on past events, as in this wave's letter).
**J. After a wrong answer:** show exactly which item(s) were correct-but-misplaced versus genuinely wrong, matching the real partial-credit structure, not a blanket "incorrect."
**K. After repeated success:** introduce passages where the requested events are not in strict chronological presentation order in the text, requiring the child to reconstruct rather than transcribe.

## Family: `wave1-fam-two-character` (RC-02, comparative)

**A. What the child needs to know:** comparison questions require evidence about BOTH sides, not a strong answer about one character and a weak or missing answer about the other.
**B. How Angel explains it:** "Treat this as two mini-questions in one: what's true of the first, and what's true of the second, using different evidence for each."
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** demonstrate building a simple two-column mental note (Character A: evidence; Character B: evidence) before writing the answer.
**D. What the child should notice:** contrast words (however, but, while, whereas) often mark exactly where the passage is drawing the comparison the question is asking about.
**E. Common traps:** writing at length about one character and only briefly or not at all about the other; describing what each character does rather than what it reveals about them.
**F. Efficient exam method:** re-read the passage once specifically looking for where each named character is mentioned, rather than trying to hold both in memory from a single read.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel prompts for evidence about the first character, checks it, then prompts separately for the second.
**H. Independent practice:** the full comparison in one unaided response.
**I. Transfer practice:** comparing two characters whose contrast is implicit (shown through behaviour) rather than explicitly stated.
**J. After a wrong answer:** identify specifically which character's evidence was missing or weak, rather than treating the whole comparison as failed.
**K. After repeated success:** extend to comparisons requiring evidence from more than one point in the passage per character, not a single obvious moment.

## Family: `wave1-fam-emotion-cause` (RC-02)

**A. What the child needs to know:** naming an emotion is only half the answer — the cause must be explained using the passage's own evidence, not a plausible-sounding general reason.
**B. How Angel explains it:** "Name the feeling first in one word. Then explain why, using what actually happens in the passage right around that moment."
**C. How Angel models the thinking:** show how to locate the specific trigger (an event, a line of dialogue, a physical description) immediately before or around the emotional moment in question.
**D. What the child should notice:** emotion is often shown indirectly, through action or physical description, rather than stated outright ("her heart was doing something urgent and uneven" rather than "she felt scared").
**E. Common traps:** naming a plausible emotion that doesn't match the specific evidence at that exact point in the passage; explaining the cause of a different, earlier emotional moment instead of the one asked about.
**F. Efficient exam method:** locate the exact moment referenced in the question first, then look immediately before and after it for the trigger, rather than searching the whole passage.
**G. Guided practice:** Angel names the emotion and asks only for the cause.
**H. Independent practice:** both the emotion and its cause, unaided.
**I. Transfer practice:** identifying an emotion shown only indirectly (through action/description) rather than named directly in nearby text.
**J. After a wrong answer:** show the specific textual trigger for the correct emotion, distinguishing it from the general mood of the passage.
**K. After repeated success:** ask about an emotional shift across a longer stretch of the passage, not a single fixed moment.

## Cross-family principles

- **Correct method before speed** (007B Part 5): every card's efficient-method step teaches a real, evidence-grounded approach, never a shortcut that risks the wrong answer for the sake of pace.
- **Supported vs independent evidence** (007B Part 11): guided attempts (steps G in every card above) must be recorded as supported evidence, distinct from independent attempts (steps H/I), matching the existing `lastAttemptSupportTier` field already used by Mathematics — no new schema required, REUSE not EXTEND.
- **No exposure of internal terminology** (007B Part 12): none of this card language ("RC-02", "QT-RC-06", "Tier 3") is learner-facing; all child-facing text above is written in the plain, age-appropriate register already established for Angel's explanations.
