# Finding: Candidate Volume & Competitive Landscape — Evidence Is Too Thin to Characterise a Trend

## 1. Official Evidence

**The one document that could answer the consortium-wide question was never read.**

- **AEP2-074** — "Total Number of CSSE 11+ Examination Candidates, 2010–2025 Entry" (csse.org.uk, published 03.04.2025). This is the only source in the entire Phase 2 evidence base that targets a genuine consortium-wide, multi-year (16-year) candidate-volume series. Phase 2 confirmed: HTTP 200 on the official CSSE domain, correct PDF type, correct/consistent file size across two independent downloads, and — by raw byte inspection — a genuine, linearized, **single-page** PDF (`/Linearized 1 ... /N 1`). It returned **zero extractable text** (`pdftotext`); this is consistent with the seven other CSSE PDFs in the same workstream that were image-only scans rather than text PDFs. **No figures from this document have been read by anyone in this programme.** Its existence, domain, file type, and single-page structure are verified; its content — the actual candidate numbers for any of the 16 years — is not (source: `research-notes/01-csse-consortium.md` item 10).

**Whole-Essex / whole-Southend aggregate figures that WERE read, and are explicitly not grammar-specific:**

- **AEP2-045** — Essex CC "Secondary School Offer Day" news article (28 Feb 2023): 16,945 total applications for September 2023 admissions, described by Essex CC as the highest number it had ever received; 84.17% offered first preference. Content was directly extracted and confirmed. This figure covers **all Essex secondary schools** — the article does not mention grammar schools, CSSE, or the Consortium by name at all (`research-notes/02-essex-cc.md` item 13).
- Southend citywide first-preference figures for 2022/2024/2025 (2,111–2,195 total applications across all 12 Southend secondaries) surfaced in web search snippets but every specific article URL returned HTTP 404 when fetched directly in Phase 2 — **these figures are unverified and are not treated as evidence** (`research-notes/03-southend-bc.md`, "Categories Searched With Nothing Found").

This is explicitly a different metric from CCHSG's own "applicants" figure above — it counts all preferences submitted via the LA's Common Application Form, not candidates who sat/registered for the CSSE test — and the 04-cchsg research note itself states the two are "not directly comparable."
  - **Internal Phase 2 conflict flagged:** `research-notes/02-essex-cc.md`'s own "Categories Searched With Nothing Found" section states that "CRGS, CCHSG, and KEGS entries in the Directory do not include an 'Applications received' line at all" — yet `research-notes/04-colchester-county-high-school-for-girls.md` (and the register entry AEP2-052) explicitly quote a CCHSG "Applications received (all preferences) Sept 2025: 739" figure from that same Directory. - **Southend's four grammar schools — single-year preference counts, no trend series.** The Secondary Schools Admissions Booklet 2026-27 (Southend-on-Sea City Council, read in full) gives "Preferences received Sept 2025" for each of the four Southend CSSE schools: Southend High School for Boys 1,077; Southend High School for Girls 896; Westcliff High School for Boys 1,035; Westcliff High School for Girls 887. This is a real, grammar-specific, per-school application figure — but only for a **single year** (Sept 2025); no prior-year preferences figures were located, so no trend can be derived (`research-notes/03-southend-bc.md` Source 4).

Offers-made data is capped by PAN by construction and does not measure application demand.

No source anywhere in the Phase 2 register provides a genuine multi-year, consortium-wide (all 7/10 schools combined) candidate or application count.

## 2. Educational Interpretation

**Consortium-wide, 16-year trend (2010–2025): insufficient evidence to characterise a trend.** The only document that could answer this (AEP2-074) was never read. There is no honest way to say whether CSSE-wide candidate numbers are rising, falling, or stable over this period — saying so would be inventing a narrative the evidence does not support. This must be stated plainly rather than inferred from adjacent figures.

**Short-term, single-school variation (CCHSG, 2022–2026): a real but narrow signal.** CCHSG's own tested-candidate count rose from 502 (2022) to 624 (2023) — a genuine year-on-year increase at one school, directly evidenced. It then appears roughly flat through 2025 (626–630, depending on which of two CCHSG documents is used), with a possible further rise to 666 in 2026 — though that 2026 figure uses a different metric ("applied" vs "tested") and may not be a like-for-like comparison. This is, at most, one school's short-term pattern across four data points with a metric-definition wrinkle on the last one and a missing year (2024). It cannot be generalised to the other 9 CSSE member schools, nor used to characterise the consortium as a whole — CCHSG is one girls' grammar in Colchester; nothing in the evidence base indicates whether Colchester Royal Grammar, KEGS, or the four Southend schools moved the same way over the same years.

**One-off anomalies, not trends:** Essex CC's 16,945-application figure (Sept 2023) was reported by Essex CC itself as "the highest number ever received" — but this is a whole-Essex, all-secondary-school statistic, driven overwhelmingly by non-selective comprehensive admissions (Essex has far more comprehensive places than the 3 Essex-administered CSSE grammar places). It cannot be read as evidence about grammar-school competitiveness specifically, and conflating the two would misrepresent the evidence. The same caution applies to Southend's citywide preference figures, which in any case were not independently verified in Phase 2 (URLs 404'd) and are excluded here.

**Where evidence conflicts:** (1) CCHSG's own two most recent admissions policies disagree with each other on the exact Sept 2025 tested/applicant counts (630/697 vs 626/726) — a small but genuine internal inconsistency in the school's own record-keeping, not reconciled by Phase 2 and not reconciled here. (2) The two Phase 2 research notes for Essex CC and CCHSG directly contradict each other about whether the Essex Directory even contains an "Applications received" line for CCHSG — one says no, the other quotes a figure from it. This should be re-checked against the primary PDF before any figure from it is relied upon with confidence.

**Overall assessment:** the evidence base for this topic is exactly as thin as the task brief predicted. What exists is: one single-school, four-to-five-point, metrically-inconsistent series (CCHSG); one single-year, four-school snapshot with no trend (Southend); and one wholly unread document that is the only source that could have answered the actual question asked. There is no basis in verified Phase 2 evidence to tell a parent whether CSSE competition is intensifying, easing, or holding steady, consortium-wide or even across most individual schools.

## 3. Implication for Angel

**Existing Angel capability reviewed:** `docs/intelligence/ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` (Sprint 5 design, not built) and `components/parent/CssePathwayParentContent.tsx` (live parent-facing code).

- **`ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` — Aligned.** The design explicitly and repeatedly forbids exactly the kind of claim this evidence base cannot support: §2 lists "How does my child compare to other applicants?" as forbidden ("no percentile or peer comparison"), and "Will my child get in? / What are our chances?" as forbidden ("no prediction of outcome"). §11 restates this as a non-goal. §12's acceptance test requires that a parent reading the page can state Angel has **not** told them their child's chances. There is no competitiveness, demand-trend, or "how hard is it to get in" framing anywhere in this document. Given how little the real evidence supports any such claim, this design's caution is not just safe — it is the only defensible position; it should not be read as merely conservative design, but as a design that happens to be quantitatively correct in a domain where a plausible-sounding competitiveness claim would in fact be unsupportable.
- **`CssePathwayParentContent.tsx` — Aligned (by absence).** Direct search of this live parent-facing component found no competitiveness, demand, oversubscription, or "chances" language at all. The only matches for "compete"/"candidate"-type strings are internal code identifiers (a `topCandidate` variable name, a UI-layout code comment about visual elements "not competing" for attention) — none are parent-facing copy. There is nothing here to correct.

No Angel capability currently makes, or proposes to make, a claim about CSSE competitiveness or rising/falling demand. There is nothing mis-aligned to fix.

**Recommendation: retain.** Both the shipped component and the unbuilt design should be left exactly as they are with respect to this topic. This finding provides no new evidence that would justify adding any competitiveness or demand-trend content — if anything, it confirms that adding such content now would be unsupportable by the available evidence and should continue to be avoided.
