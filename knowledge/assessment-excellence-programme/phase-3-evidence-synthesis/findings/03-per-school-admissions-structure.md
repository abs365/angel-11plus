# Finding: Per-School PAN, Priority-Area, and Oversubscription Structure (all 7 schools individually)

## 1. Official Evidence

**Colchester County High School for Girls (CCHSG)** — administered by Essex County Council (coordinating LA); own admissions authority is the school's Local Governing Body, part of Alpha Trust.
- PAN: 192 for September 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2027 entry (AEP2-047, AEP2-048, AEP2-049, AEP2-050, AEP2-051, AEP2-052). A FAPP Summary document (AEP2-055) states the PAN was increased by 32 places to 192 for September 2020 entry, implying a prior PAN of 160 — no official document for the pre-2020 figure was found.
- Priority Area: a 25-mile straight-line radius from the school, first appearing in the 2025-2026 policy (AEP2-049); the 2024-2025 policy (AEP2-050) has no Priority Area at all.
- Oversubscription cascade (2026 entry, AEP2-047/AEP2-048): (1) EHCP-named children scoring 320+; (2) LAC/PLAC scoring 320+; (3) up to 20 places to Pupil-Premium-eligible applicants scoring 320+; (4) up to 10 places to Service-Pupil-Premium-eligible applicants scoring 320+ within the 25-mile area; (5) remaining places up to 154 (cumulative) to within-area applicants by descending score; (6) remaining 38 to any applicant by descending score; tie-break: Pupil-Premium status, then straight-line distance.

**Colchester Royal Grammar School (CRGS)** — academy, own admissions authority (Thinking Schools Academy Trust); Essex CC is the coordinating LA.
- PAN: 128 for Year 7 entry, identical across September 2025, 2026 and 2027 (AEP2-009, AEP2-010, AEP2-011), independently corroborated by CSSE's own Information Guide (AEP2-012). Sixth Form PAN 200 (research-notes/05, Sources 1-3).
- Priority Area: **CRGS has no geographic priority area at all** — confirmed by reading all three policy years in full (research-notes/05). Its only geography-adjacent criterion is straight-line distance used as the final tie-break.
- Oversubscription cascade: (1) Admissions Priority 1 — up to 12 ring-fenced places for LAC/PLAC or Pupil-Premium-eligible applicants scoring >320, LAC/PLAC ranked first within this band; (2) Admissions Priority 2 — all remaining places by descending score to PAN; tie-break: LAC/PLAC, then Pupil-Premium, then straight-line distance.

**King Edward VI Grammar School (KEGS)** — own admissions authority (Trustees); Essex CC coordinating LA.
- PAN: 150 boys into Year 7, identical across September 2025, 2026 and 2027 entry (AEP2-016, AEP2-017, AEP2-018). A 2011 consultation letter (AEP2-022) documents a prior PAN of 112 (of which a proposed 90 would become priority-area places) — a genuine gap exists between this 2011 figure and the current 150; a commonly circulated "120→150 in 2015" claim is explicitly recorded as **unverified** (no primary document located).
- Priority Area: a 12.5-mile straight-line radius, reserving 120 of the 150 places; the residency reference date changed from 31 October in Year 6 (2025/2026 entry, AEP2-016/017/018) to 10 October in Year 6 (2027 entry, AEP2-015).
- Oversubscription cascade: places offered first to EHCP-named applicants, then by rank order of score; of the 120 priority-area places, up to 8 reserved for disadvantaged-pupil categories at a qualifying score of 330 (2026/2027 entry) or 340 (2025 entry — a confirmed year-on-year change); of the remaining 30 open places, up to 2 reserved at a qualifying score of 350; tie-break: LAC/PLAC/adopted, then Pupil Premium, then distance, then lots.

**Southend High School for Boys (SHSB)** — own admissions authority; Southend-on-Sea City Council is the coordinating LA (not Essex CC).
- PAN: 180, identical across September 2025, 2026 and 2027 entry (AEP2-001, AEP2-002, AEP2-003).
- Priority Area: fixed postcode sectors **SS0–SS9** (plus adjoining CM postcodes), shown on a shared, undated Southend-grammar-schools map (AEP2-004); this is a fixed postcode boundary, not a distance calculation.
- Oversubscription cascade (research-notes/07, Sources 1-3): (1) LAC/PLAC who passed; (2) within-area (SS0–SS9) applicants — quota up to 5/6 of PAN (150 of 180), of which 10% (15 places) reserved for preferential-consideration (FSM/Pupil-Premium) applicants; (3) outside-area applicants — remaining ~1/6 of PAN (30 places), of which 10% (3 places) reserved for preferential consideration; tie-break: preferential-consideration status, then distance, then random computerised allocation.

**Southend High School for Girls (SHSG)** — single-academy trust, own admissions authority; Southend-on-Sea City Council coordinating LA.
- PAN: **180 for September 2025 entry** (AEP2-081), rising to **224 for September 2026 and 2027 entry** (AEP2-082, AEP2-083) — a 44-place increase with no located consultation document explaining it. **The 2026 figure carries a live, unresolved Level-1-vs-Level-2 conflict — see the dedicated Finding below.**
- Priority Area: fixed postcode sectors SS0–SS9, same convention as SHSB.
- Oversubscription cascade, 5-tier (research-notes/08): (1) LAC/PLAC who passed; (2) priority-area + preferential-consideration (14 places at PAN 180 / 17 places at PAN 224); (3) other priority-area applicants (capped alone at 162 places at PAN 224 — this cap did not exist as a separate line at PAN 180, where categories 2+3 combined were capped at 144); (4) non-priority-area + preferential-consideration (3 places at PAN 180 / 4 at PAN 224); (5) remaining non-priority-area applicants (minimum 33 at PAN 180 / 41 at PAN 224); tie-break: preferential-consideration status, then distance, then random allocation.

**Westcliff High School for Boys (WHSB)** — academy, own admissions authority; Southend-on-Sea City Council coordinating LA.
- PAN: **185, stable across September 2023, 2026 and 2027 entry** (AEP2-094, AEP2-095, AEP2-096) — the only one of the 7 schools with a directly-confirmed unchanged PAN across its entire evidenced span, and explicitly confirmed unchanged by the school's own 2026/27 consultation notice ("No changes are proposed").
- Priority Area: fixed postcode sectors SS0–SS9.
- Oversubscription cascade (research-notes/09): (1) LAC/PLAC who passed; (2) within-area applicants — quota up to 80% of PAN (148 of 185), of which 10% (15 places) reserved for preferential consideration; (3) outside-area applicants — remaining places, of which 10% (4 of 37) reserved for preferential consideration; tie-break: preferential-consideration status, then distance, then random allocation.

**Westcliff High School for Girls (WHSG)** — academy, admissions authority is South East Essex Academy Trust; Southend-on-Sea City Council coordinating LA.
- PAN: **184 for September 2025 entry** (AEP2-028), and — per WHSG's own policy — **192 for September 2026 entry** (AEP2-026) and confirmed 192 for September 2027 entry (AEP2-027). **The 2026 figure carries a live, unresolved Level-1-vs-Level-1 conflict against Southend Council's own published figure of 184 — see the dedicated Finding below.**
- Priority Area: fixed postcode sectors SS0–SS9.
- Oversubscription cascade (research-notes/10): (1) LAC/PLAC who passed; (2) within-area applicants — quota "up to 147" (at PAN 184, AEP2-028) or "up to 154" (at PAN 192, AEP2-026/027), of which "up to 15" reserved for preferential consideration; (3) outside-area applicants, of which "up to 4" reserved for preferential consideration, remaining allocated by descending score; tie-break: preferential-consideration status, then distance, then random allocation.

## 2. Educational Interpretation

**Long-term trend (2023-2027 window, the well-evidenced period per the Phase 2 Readiness Report §3):** four of the seven schools show a stable PAN across every year for which official evidence exists — CRGS (128), KEGS (150), SHSB (180), WHSB (185). This is a genuine structural stability finding for those four schools specifically, not a claim that extends to years outside the evidenced window.

**Short-term variation:** three schools show a documented PAN increase within the evidenced window — CCHSG (implied 160 → 192, effective September 2020, per AEP2-055's FAPP Summary), SHSG (180 → 224, effective September 2026, per AEP2-081 vs AEP2-082/083), and WHSG (184 → 192, effective September 2026 per WHSG's own document, AEP2-026 vs AEP2-028 — though this specific figure is disputed, see Finding below). In every one of these three cases, no consultation document explaining the increase was located, despite the DfE School Admissions Code requiring public consultation before materially changed arrangements are determined (Readiness Report §5, "Weak"). This absence is recorded as a genuine evidence gap, not as evidence that no consultation occurred.

**One-off anomaly, not a trend:** KEGS's 2011 consultation letter (AEP2-022) proposing a "significant musical aptitude" admission route for up to 10% of places does not appear anywhere in the current (2025-2027) policies (research-notes/06, Conflict 2). This is recorded as a single historical proposal whose adoption or withdrawal is undocumented — not evidence of any pattern of route changes at KEGS or elsewhere.

**Priority-area mechanism is not a fluctuating variable but a structural choice, and it does not track PAN size.** CCHSG's Priority Area (25 miles) was introduced mid-run (2025-26) alongside a new Service Pupil Premium category — this is a real, dated policy change, confirmed by directly comparing AEP2-049 against AEP2-050. This should not be read as part of any broader multi-school trend: no other school in the Register shows a comparable "priority area introduced from scratch" event within the evidenced window — the other 6 schools' priority-area (or absence of one, for CRGS) is stable throughout.

**Where evidence conflicts, both live unresolved Level-1 conflicts are carried forward explicitly, not silently resolved:**
- **WHSG's PAN for September 2026 entry:** WHSG's own Determined Admissions Arrangements 2026-2027 (AEP2-026, ratified 17 March 2025) states PAN = 192. Southend-on-Sea City Council's own "Secondary Schools Admissions 2026" booklet (AEP2-031, published later, June 2025) states WHSG's PAN = 184 for the same intake. Both are official primary sources; neither the Evidence Register nor this synthesis picks one as authoritative. Educationally, this matters because it is not a rounding or typo-scale discrepancy — 8 places is a material fraction of the intake, and it changes which of the two published oversubscription-quota tables (research-notes/10, "up to 147" vs "up to 154" within-area) is actually operative for the cycle a current Year 6 family would be applying under.
- **SHSG's Determined vs third-party-mirrored 2026/27 arrangements:** the official SHSG document (AEP2-082, hosted on shsg.org) and a third-party tutoring-site mirror bearing SHSG's own branding (AEP2-084, hosted on passelevenplus.co.uk) state different ratification dates and quota figures that do not arithmetically reconcile to the stated PAN of 224 in the mirror's version. Per the evidence hierarchy, AEP2-082 (official domain) is preferred and AEP2-084 is explicitly not treated as authoritative — but the fact that a branded, plausible-looking third-party copy exists and conflicts with the official one is itself an educationally relevant finding: a parent searching online is at real risk of encountering AEP2-084-equivalent content and treating it as current.

## 3. Implication for Angel

This Finding is purely about the underlying evidence; its implication for Angel's `school`/`school_admission_threshold` schema and for the absence of per-school parent-facing content is addressed together, once, in the dedicated Finding "Implication for Angel's Admissions Schema" below, to avoid repeating the same architecture discussion seven times.

## 4. Founder Review

All PAN, priority-area, and oversubscription figures above are provisional pending Founder review of Phase 2's underlying source acquisition (per the Phase 2 Readiness Report, which itself awaited Founder approval before this Phase 3 work began). Where a figure is disputed between two official sources (WHSG, SHSG), no resolution is asserted here — the Founder or a follow-up acquisition pass would need to determine which figure is authoritative before either could be surfaced to a parent as fact. This document does not recommend which figure to use if and when the schema is populated.

# Finding: Structural Patterns Across the 7 Schools — Essex vs Southend Administration

## 1. Official Evidence

Each research-notes file independently and consistently documents which body coordinates each school's admissions and which body the school's own policy names as the geographic-criterion authority:

- **Essex-administered (Essex County Council coordinates; CRGS/CCHSG/KEGS are each their own admissions authority):** CCHSG (research-notes/04, "Administering Authority"), CRGS (research-notes/05, "Administering Authority"), KEGS (research-notes/06, "Administering Authority"). Essex CC's own Coordinated Scheme (AEP2-033) and Schools Admission Policies Directory (AEP2-036/AEP2-037/AEP2-052) confirm these are "the only 3 of 7" schools it coordinates (§7.3.5 of AEP2-033), independently corroborated by Southend's own workstream reaching the mirror-image "exactly 4 of 7" finding (AEP2-057 et al., cross-referenced in the Evidence Register's Workstream 2 and Workstream 3 compiler notes).
- **Southend-administered (Southend-on-Sea City Council coordinates; SHSB/SHSG/WHSB/WHSG are each their own admissions authority):** SHSB (research-notes/07), SHSG (research-notes/08), WHSB (research-notes/09), WHSG (research-notes/10). Confirmed via AEP2-006 and AEP2-057/AEP2-058.

Geographic-criterion mechanism, by school (all from the same sources cited in the previous Finding):
- CCHSG: 25-mile straight-line distance (AEP2-049).
- CRGS: no geographic criterion at all (research-notes/05).
- KEGS: 12.5-mile straight-line distance (AEP2-015/016/017/018).
- SHSB: fixed postcode sectors SS0–SS9 (AEP2-004; research-notes/07).
- SHSG: fixed postcode sectors SS0–SS9 (research-notes/08).
- WHSB: fixed postcode sectors SS0–SS9 (research-notes/09).
- WHSG: fixed postcode sectors SS0–SS9 (research-notes/10).

Statistical-publication asymmetry: Southend-on-Sea City Council publishes a single 47-page booklet (AEP2-031/AEP2-060/AEP2-097) giving 5 years (2021-2025) of PAN, offer-category, and appeals data for all 4 of its schools in one place. Essex County Council's equivalent Directory (AEP2-036/AEP2-037/AEP2-052) gives current-year PAN only for its 3 schools and — per the Readiness Report §7 — omits the "applications received" statistic specifically for CRGS/CCHSG/KEGS while providing it for ordinary comprehensives in the same document.

## 2. Educational Interpretation

**Genuine structural pattern (not an artefact of search depth):** the two administering authorities use two different priority-area conventions. Essex-administered schools that have a priority area at all define it as a straight-line distance radius (miles) calculated electronically by the LA (CCHSG 25 miles, KEGS 12.5 miles). Southend-administered schools uniformly define it as a fixed set of postcode sectors (SS0–SS9) illustrated on a shared map (AEP2-004). This is confirmed independently across all 7 schools' own primary documents, not inferred from a single example — it is a genuine administrative-convention difference between the two Local Authorities, not a per-school idiosyncrasy.

**A real exception within the Essex group, not a contradiction of the pattern:** CRGS has no priority area at all — it is the only one of the 7 schools with a purely score-and-tie-break structure (Priority 1 score threshold >320 for disadvantaged applicants, then rank order, distance only as the final tie-break). This is a genuine structural outlier, not a data gap — CRGS's own policy text was read in full across all three available years and contains no priority-area clause in any version (research-notes/05). It should not be characterised as "Essex schools don't use geography" (KEGS and CCHSG both do) nor folded into "the Essex convention is distance-based" without this explicit caveat.

**A secondary, near-universal pattern across all 7 schools regardless of administering authority:** every school reserves a specific sub-quota within its main categories for disadvantaged-pupil groups (Pupil Premium / Free School Meals / Service Pupil Premium / LAC-PLAC), and every school's tie-break order places disadvantaged-pupil status ahead of distance. This convention crosses the Essex/Southend divide cleanly — it is a shared design choice among all 7 schools' own admissions authorities, not an LA-imposed rule (each school states its own criteria independently).

**Data-availability asymmetry is an evidence artefact, not an educational finding about school rigour.** The Readiness Report explicitly flags this (§7, Risk 4 in §10), and this synthesis repeats the caution rather than resolving it differently: Southend-administered schools have a richer publicly available statistical base (5-year allocation and appeals tables) than Essex-administered schools, but this reflects what each Local Authority chooses to publish centrally, not anything about how rigorously CRGS, CCHSG, or KEGS individually run their own admissions.

## 3. Implication for Angel

Angel's proposed `school` schema (see next Finding) would need to represent this Essex/Southend structural difference explicitly if populated — a single generic "priority area" field storing either a distance-in-miles or a postcode-list, without a type discriminator, would silently misrepresent CRGS (which has neither) as though it had a null/zero-mile radius rather than genuinely no geographic criterion. This is a data-modelling implication only, described here for a future implementation phase to consider — not something this Finding designs or builds.

## 4. Founder Review

This structural-pattern finding is provisional and, like the school-by-school Finding above, awaits Founder review as part of the wider Phase 2/Phase 3 evidence chain. No architectural or schema decision is made here.

# Finding: The Two Unresolved Level-1 Conflicts and What They Mean for Data Reliability

## 1. Official Evidence

**Conflict 1 — WHSG PAN, September 2026 entry:**
- AEP2-026 (WHSG's own "Determined Admissions Arrangements 2026-2027," ratified by Local Governing Board 17 March 2025, hosted on whsg.info): states PAN = 192.
- AEP2-031 (Southend-on-Sea City Council "Secondary Schools Admissions 2026" booklet, version July 2025, published June 2025): states WHSG's "Published Admission Numbers for 2026" = 184.
- AEP2-030 (DfE GIAS record for WHSG, URN 136490, DfE number 882/5423) does not itself state a PAN figure and does not resolve the conflict.
- Both AEP2-026 and AEP2-031 are Level 1 Official Primary sources under the governing evidence hierarchy. Neither the Evidence Register nor the Readiness Report resolves this conflict; both explicitly record it as open (Evidence Register, Workstream 10 gaps section; Readiness Report §8, "Moderate confidence").

**Conflict 2 — SHSG Determined vs third-party mirror, 2026/27 arrangements:**
- AEP2-082 (SHSG's own "Determined Admission Arrangements 2026/27," hosted on shsg.org, ratification date stated as 31 January 2024 — the same date as the prior cycle's document, flagged by the compiler as a possible copy-paste error in the source document itself): states PAN = 224, with quotas reported in the previous Finding.
- AEP2-084 (a document hosted on passelevenplus.co.uk, a private tutoring site, bearing genuine SHSG branding, uploaded October 2025, Evidence Level 2 — Cross-Verified, explicitly NOT Level 1 because it is a third-party host): titled "Proposed Admission Arrangements 2026/27," states a different ratification date (24 September 2024) and quota figures that do not arithmetically reconcile to PAN 224.
- Per the evidence hierarchy, AEP2-082 (official domain, Level 1) is preferred over AEP2-084 (third-party host, Level 2) — this is a hierarchy-based preference, not an independent verification that AEP2-082's figures are themselves internally consistent (its own ratification date is separately flagged as a likely documentation error).

## 2. Educational Interpretation

**These are two structurally different kinds of conflict, and treating them identically would understate one and overstate the other.** The WHSG conflict (AEP2-026 vs AEP2-031) is a genuine Level-1-vs-Level-1 disagreement between two official primary sources with no hierarchy tiebreaker available — the school's own determined policy and the coordinating council's own published booklet simply disagree, and nothing in the governing evidence hierarchy says which one wins. This is the more serious of the two conflicts because it cannot be resolved by source-hierarchy reasoning alone. The SHSG conflict (AEP2-082 vs AEP2-084) does have a hierarchy tiebreaker (Level 1 beats Level 2), so it is resolved procedurally in favour of AEP2-082 — but the fact that a convincingly-branded third-party copy exists at all, with a different ratification date and non-reconciling arithmetic, is itself worth carrying forward as a standing caution about how easily a parent could encounter and trust the wrong document.

**Neither conflict should be read as evidence that CSSE admissions data in general is unreliable.** Both conflicts are isolated to specific schools and specific years (WHSG 2026 entry only; SHSG 2026/27 entry only). Every other PAN figure across all 7 schools and all evidenced years was found to be internally consistent across every source that stated it (see the school-by-school Finding above) — including cases where the same document was independently found and cross-checked by two or three different research workstreams (Readiness Report §1, "Cross-consortium documents"). The two conflicts are real but narrow; they should not be generalised into a blanket "the PAN data can't be trusted" conclusion.

**This is a short-term-variation-and-anomaly picture, not a trend.** Both conflicts concern the single most recently determined admissions cycle in the entire evidence base (2026 entry) — the cycle where consultation, ratification, and publication happen closest to the research date and where superseding/copy-paste errors are most likely to occur before a document has had time to be corrected. There is no evidence in the Register of a similar conflict for any earlier year at either school.

## 3. Implication for Angel

If a future implementation phase were to populate the proposed `school_admission_threshold` (or a new PAN-bearing table) for WHSG's or SHSG's 2026 entry cycle specifically, it could not respond to "what is this school's PAN" with a single number without either (a) silently picking a side in an unresolved Level-1 conflict, which the governing evidence-hierarchy standard explicitly prohibits, or (b) explicitly surfacing both figures with their provenance, which is a materially different (and more complex) UI/data commitment than a single clean number. This is a genuine constraint on how "ready" the evidence actually is for the two affected schools' most current cycle, even though the underlying acquisition work is otherwise complete for them.

## 4. Founder Review

Both conflicts are carried forward here exactly as recorded in Phase 2, without resolution. Resolving either — by further acquisition, by direct enquiry to the school/council, or by Founder instruction to prefer one source as a matter of policy — is a decision this synthesis does not make and does not recommend a specific resolution for.

# Finding: Implication for Angel's Admissions Schema and Parent-Facing Content

## 1. Official Evidence

- `docs/intelligence/EDUCATIONAL_INTELLIGENCE_ENGINE_V1.md` §11 (Data Model) lists `school`, `school_admission_threshold`, `consortium_threshold_fact` as status **"PROPOSED, empty schema only"**, with the explicit instruction "do not populate without real data acquisition," referencing its own §10.
- `docs/intelligence/ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` §9 (Data Model Impact) restates this verbatim: "Zero new tables required... `EDUCATIONAL_INTELLIGENCE_ENGINE_V1.md` §11 already lists `school`, `school_admission_threshold`, `consortium_threshold_fact` as PROPOSED, empty schema only — do not populate without real data acquisition. This design does not populate them." The same section states the one fact this design does surface — CSSE's consortium-wide 303 combined-score floor — is implemented as "a static, cited constant in code... not a database row," explicitly because it is "one fact, not per-school variable data."
- §9 continues: "If the Founder later wants real per-school data or a genuinely comparable mock-to-CSSE score scale, that is the trigger to populate the already-proposed `school`/`school_admission_threshold` schema — a separate, future, explicitly-authorised data-acquisition decision."
- §4 (Additional Evidence Required) of the same design document separately identifies "Score-scale non-comparability, disclosed not solved" as a genuine, unclosed gap: "Angel's own mock score... is not the same scale as CSSE's real, age-standardised combined score... This is not a data gap Angel can close — it requires CSSE's actual standardisation formula, which this platform does not hold and has no plan to acquire."
- `components/parent/CssePathwayParentContent.tsx` was read in full: it renders Child Progress, This Week, Skills Summary, Readiness Summary, Evidence Growth, Development Areas, and Recent Activity sections, all sourced from `fetchLearnerIntelligenceProfile()` and related real evidence functions. No school name, PAN figure, priority-area detail, or oversubscription-criteria content of any kind appears anywhere in this component. The only school-adjacent content is a generic link to `/learning-intelligence/parent/admissions-readiness`, described in the design document as surfacing only the single 303 consortium-wide fact, never per-school data.
- Independently confirmed via prior research (per this task's brief): no individual CSSE school name (Colchester County High School for Girls, Colchester Royal Grammar School, King Edward VI Grammar School, Southend High School for Boys, Southend High School for Girls, Westcliff High School for Boys, or Westcliff High School for Girls) currently appears anywhere in `app/` or `components/`.

## 2. Educational Interpretation

**The schema's gating condition and Phase 2's evidence do not fully overlap — they overlap for PAN and priority-area/oversubscription facts, but not for score-comparability.** The design document names two possible triggers for populating the schema: "real per-school data" or "a genuinely comparable mock-to-CSSE score scale." Phase 2 has now delivered the first trigger in full, for all 7 schools, per the school-by-school Finding above: PAN, priority-area definition, and oversubscription-category structure are documented, cited, and cross-verified for every school for the 2023-2027 window. Phase 2 has **not** delivered the second trigger at all — no standardisation formula was acquired (nor was one sought; the Readiness Report never claims to have solved this), and the design document's own framing states this is not closeable by data acquisition of the kind Phase 2 performed. This distinction matters educationally: it separates two different kinds of content the schema could hold. **Structural/administrative facts** (a school's PAN is 192; its priority area is 25 miles; its oversubscription order is X-then-Y-then-Z) are purely informational — displaying them makes no claim about any individual child's chances and carries none of the "prediction" risk the design's §5, §11, and Risks section are built to prevent. **Score-cutoff facts** (the lowest score that gained a place was 326-327 in 2024) sit much closer to that risk boundary, because a parent reading a real mock score beside a real historical cutoff is one small step from reading it as a prediction — the same risk the 303-fact placement rules in §5/§11 were written to guard against, and the two live unresolved conflicts (see the previous Finding) make this specific category of fact less safe to surface cleanly than PAN/priority-area facts are.

**Long-term / durable:** the evidence base for PAN and priority-area/oversubscription structure is now genuinely strong for all 7 schools for the 2023-2027 window (Readiness Report §11, "Ready with limitations"), and this is a stable state, not a one-off finding likely to be contradicted by further acquisition.

**Short-term / caveated:** score-cutoff depth varies sharply by school (WHSG and WHSB have multi-year series; CRGS and SHSB have only a single cross-consortium year, 2023) — any future population of `school_admission_threshold` would inherit this same unevenness and would need to represent "no data for this year" honestly rather than interpolating.

**Anomaly, not to be generalised:** the two live PAN conflicts (WHSG, SHSG) affect only their most current cycle, not the schema question in general — six of the seven schools currently have no unresolved PAN conflict for any evidenced year.

## 3. Implication for Angel

Classifying the relevant current-state Angel capabilities against this now-available evidence:

- **The empty `school` table (PAN, priority-area, oversubscription-structure fields):** **not aligned** with the evidence now available — not because the schema's shape is wrong (its shape, per §11, is well-suited to exactly this kind of per-school structural fact), but because zero rows exist while the evidence to populate all 7 schools' structural facts, for at least the 2023-2027 window, is now genuinely present and Level-1/verified.
- **The empty `school_admission_threshold` table (score-cutoff fields):** **not aligned**, and for a distinct reason from the PAN/priority-area gap above — even setting aside the empty-table fact, this category of data is inherently harder to surface safely (score-scale non-comparability, per §4 of the design, remains genuinely unsolved) and two of the seven schools carry live unresolved figures for their most recent cycle. Populating this table would need more design care than the `school` table, not merely more data entry.
- **`consortium_threshold_fact` (the single 303 CSSE-wide floor):** **partially aligned** — the fact itself is real, sourced (AEP2-005 among others, independently corroborated across at least 6 separate CSSE documents per the Readiness Report §6), and already surfaced in the Admissions Intelligence design as a static code constant rather than a database row. The underlying fact is in active use; only its database-table form remains unbuilt, and the design document explicitly explains why that particular fact does not need the table (§9: "one fact, not per-school variable data").
- **Absence of any school-specific content in `CssePathwayParentContent.tsx` or elsewhere in `app/`/`components/`:** **not aligned** with the evidence now available, in the narrow sense that the underlying facts a parent might reasonably want (their target school's PAN, its priority-area definition, its oversubscription order) are now real and citable, where before Phase 2 they were not. This is not a claim that the current absence was ever wrong given what evidence existed at the time it was built — it was correctly gated (§9's "do not populate without real data acquisition" was the right call before Phase 2) — only that the gating condition for the PAN/priority-area category of fact has now been met.

**What a future implementation phase could do, described but not built here:** populate `school` with the 7 schools' PAN-by-year, priority-area definition (with an explicit type discriminator — distance-radius vs fixed-postcode-list vs none, per the structural-pattern Finding above, so CRGS is not misrepresented), and oversubscription-category structure, each row citing its `AEP2-###` source; explicitly encode the two live PAN conflicts as recorded disagreements rather than resolved single values (e.g. two dated, sourced candidate figures per conflicted cell, not a silently-chosen one); leave `school_admission_threshold` either unpopulated or populated only with the explicit same "beside, never blended, never a prediction" framing and disclaimers the 303 fact already carries in the current design, given the additional score-scale-comparability problem that remains genuinely open; and correspondingly extend `CssePathwayParentContent.tsx` (or the existing Admissions Readiness page) with a new, clearly-labelled per-school section that never merges the 7 schools and never implies a probability of admission. None of this is implemented, redesigned, or scaffolded by this Finding.

## 4. Founder Review

Every recommendation above is provisional. In particular: this Finding does **not** infer that the Founder wants per-school content built. It states only that the specific evidentiary precondition the design document itself set — "do not populate without real data acquisition" — has now been met for PAN and priority-area/oversubscription-structure facts specifically (not for score-cutoff facts, where the precondition remains only partially met). Whether to authorise a future implementation phase, and in what form, remains entirely a Founder-approval-only architectural decision, consistent with `ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md`'s own closing line: "No implementation should begin until the Founder approves it, potentially with amendments, as a new numbered Founder Decision."
