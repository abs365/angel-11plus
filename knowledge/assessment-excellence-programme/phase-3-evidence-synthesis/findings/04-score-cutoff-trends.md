# Score Cutoff Trends & Longitudinal Patterns, 2021–2025 (and earlier years where evidenced)

**Programme:** Assessment Excellence Programme — Phase 3 (Evidence Synthesis), Workstream 4
**Status:** Draft synthesis for Founder review. Built exclusively from Phase 2's validated 101-source evidence register (`AEP2-001`..`AEP2-101`). No new web research performed.
**Scope discipline:** Each of the 7 CSSE grammar schools is treated individually throughout — never merged into a single consortium-wide series, per the governing instruction and per the Phase 2 Readiness Report's own "Asymmetric-evidence risk" warning (§10.4).

---

## Finding 0: The One Multi-Year, Multi-School Constant — the CSSE 303 Floor

### 1. Official Evidence
Across every school and every year for which any CSSE score-cutoff document was located, the in-priority-area lowest offered score sits at or extremely close to **303** — the CSSE-wide minimum standardised score below which no CSSE school may make an offer. This figure is independently confirmed, identically, across at least 6 separate CSSE documents (Phase 2 Readiness Report §6): the Age Standardisation Statement, the 2025 Entry Information Guide (`AEP2-012`/`066`/`088`), the 2023-entry Historical Guidance Sheet (`AEP2-005`), the compiled 2019–2024 Historical Guidance Sheets (`AEP2-072`/`089`/`098`), and the 2025-entry actual sheet (`AEP2-091`/`013`).

Concrete in-priority-area figures at or near the 303 floor: SHSB 2023 = 303 (`AEP2-005`); SHSG 2021–2023 = 303, 2024 = 304, 2025 = 303 (`AEP2-089`, `AEP2-090`, `AEP2-091`); WHSB 2021–2023 = 303, 2025 = 309 (`AEP2-098`); WHSG 2021–2025 = 303.02 → 303.13 → 303.60 → 303.34 → 306.71 (`AEP2-024`, school's own published table). CCHSG and KEGS carry a school-specific internal priority threshold (320/330/340/350 bands) that sits well above the 303 floor and is a different mechanism — see Findings 5 and 6.

### 2. Educational Interpretation
This is the single most reliable longitudinal fact in the entire evidence base — more reliable than any individual school's own headline cutoff, because it is corroborated by an independent CSSE-level document in nearly every year rather than resting on one school's own self-report. The pattern is: for the four schools (SHSB, SHSG, WHSB, WHSG) whose intake is not further internally sub-thresholded by the school itself, the in-priority-area cutoff is **structurally pinned at the regulatory floor, not market-determined** — i.e., "the cutoff" for the majority of each school's priority-area intake is a policy artefact of CSSE's own standardisation design, not a signal of that year's candidate strength. Genuine movement above the floor (WHSG's 306.71 in 2025, WHSB's 309 in 2025) is real and evidenced but is a single data point in each case — evidence of a possible loosening of the floor-pinning in 2025 across two schools simultaneously, which is suggestive but not, on 1–2 data points per school, a confirmed multi-year trend.

### 3. Implication for Angel
Angel's current Admissions Readiness design (`lib/learningEngine/admissionsContext.ts`) already surfaces exactly this fact — the 303 floor — as a static, sourced, disclosed constant, shown "beside, never blended" into a child's own evidence. This finding **confirms** that design choice was the right one: the 303 floor is the one number in the entire admissions landscape that is genuinely stable, multiply-corroborated, and safe to state as fact rather than trend. **Classification: aligned.** **Recommendation: retain**, unchanged.

### 4. Founder Review
This recommendation is provisional pending Founder review. No architectural change is proposed or implied by this finding; it is offered as confirming evidence for a decision already made.

## Finding 1: Southend High School for Boys (SHSB) — Insufficient Evidence to Establish Any Trend

### 1. Official Evidence
SHSB's own admissions policies (`AEP2-001`, `AEP2-002`, `AEP2-003`) confirm PAN = 180, unchanged across entry years 2025, 2026, and 2027. Exactly one official score-cutoff data point exists for SHSB in the entire acquisition: 2023 entry, Inside Priority Area = 303, Outside Priority Area = 342, from the CSSE consortium-wide table (`AEP2-005`). `research-notes/07-southend-high-school-for-boys.md` explicitly records that every other year (2016–2022, 2024, 2025–2027) was searched and no equivalent official score was found.

### 2. Educational Interpretation
With one data point, there is nothing to classify as trending up, down, or stable — this is not a "flat trend," it is an absence of a series. This must be stated plainly rather than papered over: SHSB is the thinnest score-evidence school in the programme alongside CRGS (Finding 6). The Phase 2 Readiness Report already flagged SHSB as "solid current policy, weak historical score depth" (§4) — this finding does not contradict that, it confirms it at the single-figure level.

### 3. Implication for Angel
No Angel capability currently surfaces or references SHSB-specific score data (confirmed by Phase 3 kickoff's full-tree search — no individual CSSE school name appears in learner/parent-facing app code). There is nothing to classify as aligned or misaligned because nothing exists to compare. **Classification: not applicable (no current Angel capability touches this).** **Recommendation: retain** — i.e. do not attempt to build a per-school SHSB trend view; the evidence does not support one.

### 4. Founder Review
Provisional. Any future decision to acquire deeper SHSB score history (e.g. via a targeted FOI request, mirroring what WHSB's own governors disclosed) is a Founder-approval acquisition decision, not something this synthesis recommends or assumes.

## Finding 2: Southend High School for Girls (SHSG) — Stable Inside-Priority Floor, Noisy Outside-Priority Band, and a Major Unexplained PAN Anomaly

### 1. Official Evidence
Score series (CSSE "last selective place offered," Inside/Outside Priority Area), all from official CSSE documents (`AEP2-089` compiled sheet for 2021–2023; `AEP2-090` for 2024; `AEP2-091`/`AEP2-013` for 2025):

| Entry Year | Inside Priority Area | Outside Priority Area |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 303 | 318 |
| 2022 | 303 | 311 |
| 2023 | 303 | 324 |
| 2024 | 304 | 327 |
| 2025 | 303 | 321 |

2019 and 2020 exist only as 4-year rolling "guidance bands" (both years: in-area 303, out-of-area 303 — Source `AEP2-089`), not single-year actuals, and are reported separately per that document's own explicit caveat rather than treated as comparable to the 2021–2025 figures.

PAN: 180 for September 2025 entry (`AEP2-081`); **224** for September 2026 and 2027 entry (`AEP2-082`, `AEP2-083`) — a jump of 44 places. `research-notes/08-southend-high-school-for-girls.md` confirms this was searched for explicitly and no consultation document explaining the increase was found, despite the DfE School Admissions Code requiring public consultation before determining materially changed arrangements. A third-party mirror of a "Proposed" (not "Determined") 2026/27 document (`AEP2-084`) carries different quotas that do not arithmetically reconcile to PAN 224, and is explicitly recorded as non-authoritative, not silently resolved.

### 2. Educational Interpretation
Two genuinely distinct patterns exist here and must not be conflated:
- **Short-term variation, not a trend:** the outside-priority-area score moved 318 → 311 → 324 → 327 → 321 across 2021–2025 — a 16-point range with no monotonic direction. This is normal year-to-year admissions noise for a fixed-size intake, not a directional trend. The inside-priority figure is pinned to the 303 CSSE floor in 4 of 5 years, with one single-point deviation to 304 in 2024 that has no available explanation and should not be over-read.
- **A genuine one-off anomaly, evidenced but unexplained:** the PAN increase from 180 to 224 is a real, dated, primary-sourced fact — not a data-quality artefact — but its *cause* is completely unevidenced. This is exactly the kind of "one-off anomaly" the governing instruction requires calling out explicitly rather than folding into a "score trend" narrative. Critically, because the PAN increase takes effect for 2026 entry and 2026's National Offer Day has not yet occurred at research date, **there is no score data yet for the enlarged intake** — meaning nothing in the current score series can yet demonstrate what effect (if any) the 44-place PAN increase will have on the cutoff. Any claim that "SHSG's cutoff will fall because the school got bigger" would be pure speculation not supported by any evidence in this register.

### 3. Implication for Angel
No Angel capability currently surfaces SHSG-specific data. **Classification: not applicable.** **Recommendation: retain** current non-school-specific design. If a future Founder decision authorises building school-specific admissions content, SHSG is the clearest example in the whole dataset of why real per-school data must never be silently smoothed into a single "trend" narrative — the PAN anomaly alone would make any predictive framing actively misleading to a parent.

### 4. Founder Review
Provisional. The 180→224 PAN conflict and its unexplained cause are carried forward as an open question per the Phase 2 Readiness Report's instruction (§10.2) — not resolved here, not silently picked one way.

## Finding 3: Westcliff High School for Boys (WHSB) — Deepest Historical Series in the Programme, Two Disjoint Datasets, a Genuine Mid-Series Gap

### 1. Official Evidence
Two distinct, methodologically different official series exist for WHSB, and must be reported separately per `research-notes/09-westcliff-high-school-for-boys.md`'s own explicit warning not to conflate them:

**(A) WHSB's own FOI disclosure (`AEP2-099`), outside-priority-area, precise decimal:**
| Entry Year | Score |
|---|---|
| 2015 | 325.394 |
| 2016 | 324.886 |
| 2017 | 325.327 |
| 2018 | 332.764 |

**(B) CSSE "last selective place offered" (`AEP2-098`), Inside/Outside Priority Area:**
| Entry Year | Inside | Outside |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 303 | 353 |
| 2022 | 303 | 349 |
| 2023 | 303 | 349 |
| 2024 | *not found — confirmed gap* | *not found* |
| 2025 | 309 | 351 |

2019 and 2020 exist only as rolling 4-year bands (in-area 303/308/310 for 2020, 303/303/303 for 2019; out-of-area 324/344/353 for 2020, 324/333/341 for 2019 — `AEP2-098`), not single-year actuals. PAN: 185, confirmed stable across 2023, 2026, and 2027 (`AEP2-094`, `AEP2-095`, `AEP2-096`), with the school's own 2026/27 consultation notice explicitly stating "No changes are proposed from the admission arrangements which are currently in place."

### 2. Educational Interpretation
Within dataset (A), the outside-priority score is essentially flat around 325 for three consecutive years (2015–2017: 325.4, 324.9, 325.3 — a range of under 0.5 points, genuine short-term stability) followed by a real, evidenced jump to 332.8 in 2018 (+7.4 points in one year) — a one-off single-year movement, not (on 4 points) enough to call a "long-term rising trend," but a real and larger-than-typical year-on-year change worth naming as such.

Within dataset (B), the outside-priority score sits in the 349–353 band across 2021–2023 and 2025 (no clear direction, normal variation), while the inside-priority score — pinned at the 303 floor for 2021–2023 — moves to 309 in 2025, mirroring the same single-point-of-departure-from-the-floor pattern seen at SHSG (Finding 2) and WHSG (Finding 4) in the identical year. This is a real, cross-school pattern worth flagging as an observation (three of four Southend-administered schools show their first floor-departure in or around 2024–2025) but with only one data point per school it cannot be called a confirmed multi-year trend — it is, honestly, either the start of a genuine trend or a single unusual year, and the evidence does not yet distinguish between the two.

The **gap at 2024** (dataset B) and the **gap at 2019–2020** (both datasets, replaced by non-comparable rolling bands) mean WHSB's "5-year 2021–2025 series" is genuinely a 4-point series with a hole in it, not 5 continuous years — this must be stated honestly rather than interpolated.

### 3. Implication for Angel
No Angel capability currently surfaces WHSB-specific data. **Classification: not applicable.** **Recommendation: retain** current design; WHSB's data — the richest in the programme — still does not support a clean, presentable "5-year trend line" without either fabricating the 2024 figure or visually representing a gap, which is itself a design complexity worth flagging if per-school historical display is ever authorised.

### 4. Founder Review
Provisional. No recommendation to acquire the missing 2024 figure is made here — that is a Founder-authorised acquisition decision (e.g. targeted FOI), consistent with the Phase 2 Readiness Report's Recommendation §11.

## Finding 4: Westcliff High School for Girls (WHSG) — Cleanest Self-Published Series in the Programme, Modest Floor-Departure in 2025, and a Live, Unresolved PAN Conflict for 2026

### 1. Official Evidence
WHSG publishes its own 5-year lowest-offered-score table directly on its website (`AEP2-024`) — the only school in the programme to do so unprompted, in this form:

| Entry Year | Priority Area (lowest offered) | Out of Priority Area (lowest offered) | Out of Priority Area (where places eventually offered) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 303.02 | 344.04 | 323.27 |
| 2022 | 303.13 | 327.06 | 320.00 |
| 2023 | 303.60 | 342.00 | 322.00 |
| 2024 | 303.34 | 346.92 | 331.12 |
| 2025 | 306.71 | 345.87 | — |

PAN: 184 for 2021–2025 (implied by the Southend LA booklet's allocation table, `AEP2-031`, and stated explicitly for 2025 in `AEP2-028`, §4). For 2026 entry, **a live, unresolved Level-1-vs-Level-1 conflict exists**: WHSG's own "Determined Admissions Arrangements 2026-2027" (`AEP2-026`), ratified 17 March 2025, states PAN = 192; Southend-on-Sea City Council's own booklet (`AEP2-031`), published later (June 2025), states WHSG's PAN for 2026 = 184. WHSG's own website (`AEP2-024`) separately describes 192 as "a variation from the published admission number," which is itself ambiguous about which figure is the true PAN. This conflict is recorded, not resolved, per the Phase 2 evidence-hierarchy standard.

### 2. Educational Interpretation
This is the cleanest genuine 5-year series in the entire dataset, and it supports a real, if modest, observation: the priority-area figure is essentially flat at the 303 floor for four consecutive years (303.02, 303.13, 303.60, 303.34 — a range of 0.58 points, textbook short-term noise around a fixed floor), then moves to 306.71 in 2025 — a real, evidenced, single-year step of +3.37 points off the floor. This is the same 2025 floor-departure pattern seen independently at SHSG and WHSB (Finding 2, Finding 3) — three schools, same admissions year, same direction. Named together, this is the strongest candidate for a genuine emerging pattern in the whole dataset, but it remains **one data point per school** — the evidence supports saying "worth watching in 2026," not "confirmed trend."

The out-of-priority-area figures show no clear direction (327.1 to 346.9, noisy) and the "eventually offered" sub-column sits meaningfully below the headline offer-day figure every year (by 15–21 points) — a real, distinct metric (reflecting declined offers cascading down the list) that must never be conflated with the headline "lowest offered" figure when presenting this data.

The PAN conflict is structurally significant, not cosmetic: a parent using a 192-place PAN to judge their child's odds versus a 184-place PAN is working with an 8-place (4.3%) difference in intake size for the exact year that also shows the only floor-departure in the 5-year series — meaning any attempt to explain *why* the cutoff moved in 2025 by reference to intake size would itself be standing on unresolved ground for the following year.

### 3. Implication for Angel
No Angel capability currently surfaces WHSG-specific data. **Classification: not applicable.** **Recommendation: retain** current design. If per-school historical ranges are ever authorised (see Finding 8), WHSG's data is the strongest single candidate to build a first prototype against — but the live PAN conflict means even this richest series cannot currently be presented to a parent as settled fact.

### 4. Founder Review
Provisional. The WHSG PAN conflict (184 vs 192) is carried forward as an explicit open question, consistent with Phase 2 Readiness Report §10.2 — this synthesis does not pick a side.

## Finding 5: Colchester County High School for Girls (CCHSG) — A Real, Documented Policy Change and an Apparent Downward Cutoff Movement That Is Not Methodologically Comparable Across It

### 1. Official Evidence
PAN: 192, confirmed stable across entry years 2024, 2025, 2026, 2027 (`AEP2-047` to `AEP2-051`). Historically, PAN was raised by 32 places (from an implied 160) to 192 effective September 2020, per a DfE-branded Fair Access and Partnership Plan document (`AEP2-055`) — the only source in the whole programme evidencing a specific pre-2020 CCHSG figure.

A real, dated, documented structural policy change occurred between the 2024-25 and 2025-26 cycles: the 2024-2025 policy (`AEP2-050`) has **no** 25-mile Priority Area and **no** Service Pupil Premium (SPP) category; the 2025-2026 policy (`AEP2-049`) onward introduces both. No consultation document evidencing the reasoning was found (`research-notes/04`, Gaps section), despite this being a materially changed arrangement under the DfE School Admissions Code.

Lowest-offered/cut-off score, from CCHSG's own "National Offer Day" FAQ (`AEP2-053`) and cross-checked against a second CCHSG FAQ (`AEP2-054`):

| Entry Year | Cut-off (within priority area / where applicable) | Cut-off (outside area) | Lowest score that actually gained a place |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 333–334 (single figure — Priority Area did not exist yet) | — | 326–327 |
| 2024 | 329–330 | — | 326–327 |
| 2025 | 321–322 (`AEP2-053`) / 323–324 (`AEP2-054`, internal discrepancy) | 332–333 | 318–319 (both sources agree) |
| 2026 | 326–327 (planning figure — offers not yet made at research date) | 332–333 | not yet available |

### 2. Educational Interpretation
There is a real, evidenced downward movement in the headline cut-off across 2023→2024→2025 (333–334 → 329–330 → 321–322/323–324) — a genuine multi-year direction, not noise, because it moves consistently in one direction across three consecutive years rather than oscillating. However, this must be qualified honestly: the *metric itself changed shape* mid-series — 2023 and 2024 report a single undivided cut-off (no Priority Area existed), while 2025 onward reports a Priority-Area-split figure. The 2025 within-area figure (321–324) is answering a subtly different question ("lowest score among Priority Area applicants") than the 2023/2024 figure ("lowest score among all applicants"), so **the apparent 3-year decline is partly a real score movement and partly a metric-definition change**, and the evidence does not allow these two effects to be separated. This is exactly the kind of surface-level "trend" that would overclaim if presented without this caveat — it should be described as "a real, falling number across 3 years, of which an unknown portion reflects a genuine change in the metric being measured," not as a clean 3-year cutoff decline.

Two further honest flags: an internal discrepancy exists between CCHSG's own two current publications on the exact 2025 within-area figure (321–322 vs 323–324) — both are the school's own documents, not reconciled, and should not be silently picked between. And the 2026 figure is a forward planning projection, not yet an outcome — offers had not been made at research date.

### 3. Implication for Angel
No Angel capability currently surfaces CCHSG-specific data. **Classification: not applicable.** **Recommendation: retain** current design. CCHSG is the clearest cautionary example in this dataset of why raw year-over-year cutoff comparison is dangerous without disclosing underlying policy changes — any future per-school feature must carry this exact caveat if CCHSG data is ever shown.

### 4. Founder Review
Provisional. The missing consultation document for the Priority Area/SPP change, and the internal 321-322-vs-323-324 discrepancy, are both carried forward as open items, not resolved here.

## Finding 6: Colchester Royal Grammar School (CRGS) and King Edward VI Grammar School (KEGS) — Contrast Case: Structurally Thin Score Evidence

### 1. Official Evidence
**CRGS:** PAN = 128, word-for-word identical policy text across 2025, 2026, and 2027 entry (`AEP2-009` to `AEP2-011`) — confirmed by direct comparison of all three PDFs, not merely assumed stable. The only score-cutoff figure found for CRGS in the entire programme is the 2023-entry consortium-wide table figure: Outside Priority Area = 340 (`AEP2-005`; Colchester schools have no Priority Area distinction in that table). No CRGS-specific score data exists for any other year (`research-notes/05`, Categories Searched section).

**KEGS:** PAN = 150, stable across 2025, 2026, 2027 entry (`AEP2-015` to `AEP2-018`). Historically, PAN was 112 in 2011 (a genuine consultation letter, `AEP2-022`, pre-dating this programme's 2016–2026 window but retained as context) — a claimed intermediate step to "120→150 effective September 2015" circulates on third-party sites but is **explicitly unverified against any primary source** and is not reported as fact. The only score-cutoff figure found for KEGS is 2025 entry only: in-area 354, out-of-area 367, from the school's own FAQ page verbatim quote (`AEP2-020`). Separately, KEGS's internal disadvantaged-pupil qualifying threshold (a sub-quota mechanism, not the headline cutoff) changed from 340 (2025 entry) to 330 (2026 and 2027 entry) — confirmed genuine and dated by comparing the policy PDFs directly (`AEP2-016` vs `AEP2-018`), though no consultation document explaining it was found.

### 2. Educational Interpretation
Both schools have exactly **one** headline score data point each in the entire 2016–2026 window (CRGS: 2023 only; KEGS: 2025 only). This is not a thin trend — it is no trend at all, and must be stated as such rather than extrapolated from a single figure. This stands in sharp, and evidence-confirmed, contrast to WHSG's clean 5-year series (Finding 4) and even SHSG's/WHSB's partial 5-year series (Findings 2–3). The Phase 2 Readiness Report already identified the root cause structurally, not as a research failure of this synthesis: Essex County Council (which administers CRGS, CCHSG, and KEGS) omits the "applications received" statistic specifically for its 3 selective schools while providing it for ordinary comprehensives in the same document, and publishes no multi-year back-series for any of its 3 grammar schools — whereas Southend-on-Sea City Council (administering SHSB, SHSG, WHSB, WHSG) publishes a 5-year back-series for all 4 of its grammar schools in a single booklet. This is a genuine asymmetry in what the two administering authorities publish, not evidence that Essex-administered schools are less rigorously run or more/less selective — that distinction must be preserved explicitly whenever this contrast is presented (Phase 2 Readiness Report §10.4, "Asymmetric-evidence risk").

KEGS's 340→330 threshold change is real but must not be mistaken for the headline cutoff falling — it is a sub-quota qualifying threshold for disadvantaged-pupil places specifically, a narrower and different mechanism than the general lowest-offered-score figure, and no comparable headline-score history exists for KEGS to say whether the general cutoff moved at all.

### 3. Implication for Angel
No Angel capability currently surfaces CRGS or KEGS data. **Classification: not applicable.** **Recommendation: retain** current design. If Angel were ever to build a per-school comparison feature (which the current design explicitly excludes, see Finding 8), CRGS and KEGS could not be included on equal footing with the Southend-administered schools without either fabricating missing years or conspicuously showing a 1-point "series," which would itself misrepresent the schools as somehow less examined than their Southend counterparts — this is a genuine design risk to flag now, before any such feature is considered, not an incidental footnote.

### 4. Founder Review
Provisional. No recommendation is made to pursue further acquisition (e.g. targeted FOI requests to CRGS/KEGS) — that remains a Founder-authorised decision per the Phase 2 Readiness Report's Recommendation §11.

## Finding 7: Cross-School Synthesis — What the 2021–2025 Window Actually Supports, and What It Does Not

### 1. Official Evidence
Summarising Findings 1–6 without adding new claims: of the 7 schools, 2 (SHSB, CRGS) have exactly one score data point in the target window; 1 (KEGS) has exactly one headline data point plus a separately-tracked sub-threshold; 1 (CCHSG) has a 4-year series (2023–2026, with 2026 unrealised and a metric-definition change mid-series); and 3 (SHSG, WHSB, WHSG) have genuine multi-year series of 4–5 points, each with at least one internal gap, conflict, or non-comparable-metric caveat already documented in Findings 2–4.

### 2. Educational Interpretation
No school in this programme has an evidence base sufficient to support a claim of a confirmed **long-term** trend (defined here, conservatively, as a consistent multi-year direction with no gaps and no metric changes) — the richest series (WHSG, 5 points) shows 4 flat years followed by 1 higher year, which is a real observation but is one data point of possible-change, not a demonstrated trend. Three schools independently show their first floor-departure in the same 2025 admissions year (SHSG 304 in 2024 is the exception/earlier signal; WHSG 306.71 and WHSB 309 both land in 2025) — this cross-school coincidence is worth naming explicitly as an observation for future monitoring, but the Phase 2 Readiness Report's own standing caution applies directly here: 3–5 years of data is not enough to prove long-term direction, and Phase 3 must not present the well-evidenced 2023–2027 window as though it licenses trend claims the underlying point-counts cannot support. The honest overall statement is: **the 2021–2025 window shows real short-term variation and at least three genuine one-off anomalies (SHSG's PAN jump, WHSG's PAN conflict, CCHSG's Priority-Area introduction), but not one single confirmed long-term trend, for any of the 7 schools.**

### 3. Implication for Angel
**Classification: aligned.** Angel's current Admissions Intelligence design contains zero per-school trend claims, zero predictive framing, and zero peer-comparison content (`ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` §2, §11) — which this evidence base actively supports rather than merely permits, since the underlying data genuinely cannot sustain trend claims for most schools. **Recommendation: retain.**

### 4. Founder Review
Provisional. This is a synthesis conclusion, not a new instruction; no design change is proposed.

## Finding 8: Does Angel's Non-Predictive Admissions Intelligence Design Still Hold Up?

### 1. Official Evidence
`ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` §2 and §11 explicitly exclude: offer prediction ("Will my child get in?"), peer/percentile comparison, and school-choice/School Comparison guidance, from Angel's current Admissions Readiness feature. §9 confirms the `school`/`school_admission_threshold` schema is "PROPOSED, empty schema only — do not populate without real data acquisition," and that the single 303 figure lives as a disclosed, sourced code constant rather than per-school database rows. `lib/learningEngine/admissionsContext.ts` (read directly for this synthesis) confirms this is implemented exactly as designed: `CSSE_COMBINED_SCORE_FLOOR = 303`, shown with a mandatory disclaimer ("not a score your child has been given, not a target to reach, and not a prediction... Angel's own scores are not on the same scale as CSSE's real, age-standardised result"). `CssePathwayParentContent.tsx` confirms no historical score-range content exists anywhere in the current parent-facing UI.

### 2. Educational Interpretation
The question posed by this workstream's brief is whether this conservative design is now *overly* cautious given that real historical ranges "could be shown safely without turning into prediction." Findings 0–7 answer this directly and, on balance, **do not support loosening the design**:

- The only genuinely stable, safely-presentable multi-year fact in the entire evidence base is the 303 floor itself — which Angel already shows.
- Every school's individual score series carries at least one live, unresolved conflict (WHSG's PAN), one unexplained anomaly (SHSG's PAN, CCHSG's policy change), a mid-series metric-definition change (CCHSG), a real gap (WHSB's 2024), or is simply too thin to show as a "series" at all without misleadingly implying more evidence exists than does (SHSB, CRGS, KEGS).
- The Southend/Essex asymmetry (Finding 6) means any per-school display would structurally make 4 schools look "well-documented" and 3 look "thin," which is an evidence-availability artefact, not an educational fact about those schools — a real risk of misleading a parent even with entirely accurate, cited figures.
- Showing a genuine historical range (e.g. WHSG's 303–306.71) invites exactly the reading Angel's design is built to prevent — a parent comparing their own child's mock score against a real school's historical range is one small step from treating it as a personal prediction, even with a disclaimer, especially under exam-anxiety conditions (`ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` §12, dimension 5, "Wellbeing protection" — explicitly flagged as needing "extra scrutiny" for exactly this reason).

That said, one narrow, genuinely low-risk enhancement is visible in the evidence and worth naming honestly rather than omitting: **the 303 floor itself could be shown per-relevant-school rather than only consortium-wide**, since it is confirmed identical everywhere the school data exists and does not introduce any of the volatility problems above (it is not a "how competitive was this school" figure, it is a fixed regulatory floor). This is a much smaller, safer step than showing full historical ranges, and even this is offered as an option for Founder consideration, not a recommendation to build.

### 3. Implication for Angel
| Angel capability | Classification | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 303 floor shown as static, sourced, disclosed constant, beside (not blended into) Readiness | Aligned | Retain |
| Exclusion of offer prediction | Aligned | Retain |
| Exclusion of peer/percentile comparison | Aligned | Retain |
| Exclusion of School Comparison / school-choice guidance | Aligned | Retain |
| Empty `school`/`school_admission_threshold` schema (not populated) | Aligned | Retain |
| Per-school historical score-range display (does not currently exist) | N/A — not built | Do not build, on current evidence |

No capability in this review is classified as **not aligned**, **partially aligned**, or in need of **strengthen**, **replace**, **hide**, or **retire** — the evidence in this synthesis affirms the existing conservative design rather than identifying a gap in it.

### 4. Founder Review
This entire finding, including the narrow "303-floor-per-school" option named above, is provisional and offered for Founder consideration only. This synthesis does not implement, prototype, or redesign anything. Whether to pursue even the narrow option — or to leave the design exactly as it is — remains a Founder-approval-only architectural decision. No inference is made here about what the Founder will decide.
