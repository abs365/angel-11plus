**Programme:** Angel 11+ Assessment Excellence Programme — Phase 3 (Evidence Synthesis)
**Workstream:** 06 — Evidence Reliability & Policy-Change Patterns (meta-finding, cross-cutting)
**Status:** Draft finding for Founder review. Synthesis only — no new sources acquired, no Phase 2 source re-verified.
**Scope discipline:** This finding is about the *reliability and volatility of the evidence itself*, not about any single school's structure or score history (see Workstreams 3 and 4 for that). ## 1. Official Evidence

All facts below are drawn from the Phase 2 Evidence Register (`ASSESSMENT_EXCELLENCE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md`) and its underlying research notes, cited to AEP2-### IDs. No new sources were consulted.

**A. CCHSG — a documented mid-run policy change with no located consultation trail.**
- The A1 Admissions Policy 2024-2025 (AEP2-050, reviewed Nov 2022, approved by LGB Mar 2023) has **no** 25-mile Priority Area and **no** Service Pupil Premium (SPP) reserved-places category. Oversubscription was simply LAC/PLAC (≥320) → up to 20 Pupil Premium places (≥320) → remaining places by rank order.
- The very next policy year, A1 Admissions Policy 2025-2026 (AEP2-049, reviewed Apr 2023, approved by LGB Feb 2024), introduces a 25-mile Priority Area and a new "up to 10 places for SPP-eligible applicants within that area" category, restructuring the full oversubscription cascade to seven tiers.
- No consultation document, minutes, or notice evidencing this change was located by the workstream, despite the DfE School Admissions Code requiring public consultation before admission authorities determine materially changed arrangements (AEP2-050/049 gap note; Readiness Report §5).
- PAN itself has been stable at 192 across all four captured policy years (2024-2027) — the volatility here is in the *criteria structure*, not the headline number (AEP2-047/048/049/050/051).

**B. SHSG — a 44-place PAN increase between consecutive entry years, again with no consultation document found.**
- SHSG's own Determined Admission Arrangements 2025/26 (AEP2-081, ratified 31 Jan 2024) states PAN = 180, independently corroborated by CSSE's own 2025-Entry Information Guide (AEP2-088/012/066).
- SHSG's own Determined Admission Arrangements 2026/27 (AEP2-082, ratified 31 Jan 2024 per its own key-dates table) states PAN = 224 — a 44-place (24%) increase for the very next entry year.
- The workstream searched specifically for a consultation document explaining this increase and found none — only the bare fact of the increase in the two determined-arrangements PDFs, and a "Last consulted... 31 January 2024" date with no linked consultation text or rationale (research-notes/08, "Categories Searched With Nothing Found").
- The internal oversubscription-quota cascade was restructured in the same policy transition (Cat.2 14→17, new standalone Cat.3 cap of 162, Cat.4 3→4, Cat.5 floor 33→41) — AEP2-081/082, register Key Facts table.

**C. KEGS — two concurrent changes between the 2026 and 2027 policy years.**
- The Priority-Area residency reference date changed from **31st October in Year 6** (2025 and 2026 entry policies, AEP2-018/AEP2-016/017) to **10th October in Year 6** (2027 entry policy, AEP2-015) — confirmed by independently reading both PDFs in full.
- The qualifying standardised-score threshold for the disadvantaged-pupil sub-quota within the Priority Area changed from **340** (2025 entry, AEP2-018) to **330** (2026 and 2027 entry, AEP2-017/AEP2-015).
- No KEGS-specific consultation document was found for either change (research-notes/06, "Categories Searched With Nothing Found").
- By contrast, KEGS's headline PAN (150) has been stable across all three captured policy years (AEP2-015/016/017/018) — again, volatility is concentrated in the *mechanics* of the Priority Area, not the topline admission number.

**D. Two live, unresolved Level-1-vs-Level-1 conflicts, both concerning the current/upcoming admissions cycle.**
- **WHSG PAN, September 2026 entry:** WHSG's own "Determined Admissions Arrangements 2026-2027" (AEP2-026, ratified 17 March 2025) states explicitly "the School's Published Admission Number (PAN) is 192." Southend-on-Sea City Council's own "Secondary Schools Admissions 2026" booklet (AEP2-031/060, published June 2025 — later than the school's document) states WHSG's "Published Admission Numbers for 2026" as 184, in the same table used identically for all 12 Southend secondary schools. WHSG's own website (AEP2-024) muddies this further, describing 192 as "a variation from the published admission number for this year group" — language that itself implies 192 may not be the PAN. This is recorded as unresolved in the register (AEP2-026/031 Notes) and in research-notes/10 ("Possible Conflicts," item 1); neither figure has been picked as authoritative.
- **SHSG 2026/27 arrangements, official vs third-party mirror:** SHSG's own site hosts "Determined Admission Arrangements 2026/27" (AEP2-082) stating ratification 31 Jan 2024 and quotas summing consistently to PAN 224. A third-party tutoring-site mirror, passelevenplus.co.uk (AEP2-084, Level 2/Cross-Verified, not Level 1), hosts a document with the same SHSG branding but titled "Proposed Admission Arrangements 2026/27," a different ratification date (24 Sept 2024), and quota figures that do not arithmetically reconcile to PAN 224 on multiple readings. AEP2-084 is explicitly recorded as non-authoritative per the evidence hierarchy (official domain preferred), but the discrepancy itself — a real document circulating with SHSG's own branding and materially different figures — is not resolved, only flagged (register AEP2-084 Notes; research-notes/08 "Possible Conflicts," item 1).

**E. CSSE's own Publication Scheme states 10 member schools, not the 7 this programme scopes.**
- AEP2-070, independently re-fetched and confirmed by the Phase 2 compiler, states verbatim: "The ten schools that are currently members of the CSSE are as follows: Colchester County High School, Colchester Royal Grammar School, King Edward VI Grammar School, **Shoeburyness High School**, Southend High School for Boys, Southend High School for Girls, **St Bernard's High School**, **St Thomas More High School**, Westcliff High School for Boys, Westcliff High School for Girls." This is a live, official, current document from CSSE's own domain — not a historical artefact — and directly disputes the "7 grammar schools" framing used throughout this programme's scope (research-notes/01, "Possible Conflicts," item 1; Readiness Report §9 item 4).

**F. Contrasting stability finding, for calibration.** Not every admissions fact moves. The CSSE-wide minimum standardised-score floor of 303 is independently confirmed, identically, across at least six separate CSSE documents spanning different years and workstreams (AEP2-005, AEP2-012, AEP2-081/082/083 references, AEP2-088, AEP2-090, AEP2-091 — see register entry AEP2-005 and Readiness Report §6), and SHSG's own oversubscription-cascade text states this floor exists "to ensure the entrance standard remains comparable to that which was applied in the period 1990 to 1994" — i.e. the *mechanism* is deliberately designed for multi-decade stability, and the evidence confirms it has in fact held stable across every year this acquisition captured (2021-2025 actuals, with one single-point deviation at SHSG 2024-entry, 304 vs 303, unexplained — AEP2-012, research-notes/08 "Possible Conflicts," item 3).

## 2. Educational Interpretation

**This is not one pattern — it is at least three distinct kinds of change, and they carry different weight.**

**Long-term / structural trend: none directly evidenced.** The evidence base does not support a claim that CSSE admissions policy "trends" in any particular direction year over year — it is too thin before 2023 for any of the 7 schools except CCHSG (which has one indirect historical PAN clue, AEP2-055, implying 160→192 around 2020) and KEGS (one 2011 consultation letter, AEP2-022/AEP2-008, showing 112 places pre-priority-area). What the evidence *does* support is a structural observation that holds across every school examined: **the mechanics of oversubscription (priority-area boundaries, residency-date cut-offs, disadvantaged-pupil thresholds, quota splits) change considerably more often, and more materially, than the headline PAN.** CCHSG's PAN held at 192 across four years while its criteria structure was rebuilt; KEGS's PAN held at 150 while its residency date and threshold both moved between adjacent years. This is a genuine cross-school pattern, observed independently at two different schools under two different administering authorities (Essex CC for both, in fact — see caveat below), not a one-off.

**Short-term / year-to-year variation: the norm, not the exception, for exactly the fields Angel would most want to show a parent.** In the years this acquisition captured with enough depth to compare adjacent policy documents directly (2023-2027 for most schools), every school examined in that depth had at least one material year-on-year change in its criteria mechanics (CCHSG: Priority Area + SPP category added; KEGS: residency date and threshold both changed; SHSG: PAN itself changed by 44 places; WHSG: quota sub-splits changed between the 184-PAN and 192-PAN policy eras). Given that 4 of the 7 named schools show at least one such change within a 3-4 year observation window, "admissions criteria are materially revised most years" is a reasonable characterisation of the observed rate — not proven as a universal law across all 7 schools (CRGS and WHSB show no equivalent captured change in this acquisition), but strong enough that Angel should not assume any single school's arrangement is durable year to year without checking.

**One-off anomaly, treated as such and not generalised:** the SHSG 2024-entry lowest-offered score sitting at 304 rather than the otherwise-universal 303 floor (Item F above) is a single-point deviation with no explanation available in the primary sources — it is reported as an observed fact, not evidence of a wider pattern of the 303 floor itself being unstable. The 303 floor's stability across six independent documents and every other year observed is the stronger, better-evidenced pattern.

**The two unresolved conflicts explained, side by side, without concealment:**
- *WHSG PAN 2026:* the school itself says 192; the coordinating council, in a document published *later* than the school's, says 184; the school's own website hedges by calling 192 "a variation" from an unstated baseline PAN. This is not a case of an old document versus a new one — both are current, both are official-primary, and they disagree about a hard number for the same intake year. It matters because a PAN is exactly the kind of number a parent would use to judge how competitive a year is, and Angel could not currently state it correctly with certainty for this school's 2026 cohort.
- *SHSG 2026/27 arrangements — official vs mirror:* here the disagreement is between an official-domain document and a third-party site that nonetheless carries genuine SHSG branding and could easily be mistaken for authoritative by a parent searching the open web (which is exactly how the Phase 2 workstream itself found it). The educational risk is different in kind from the WHSG case: it is not "which official body is right," it is "a parent doing their own research is likely to encounter a plausible-looking but wrong document, and no signal on the page itself tells them so."

**The CSSE 7-vs-10-school question is a scope-definition issue, not a volatility issue** — it does not change year to year, it is simply a live disagreement between this programme's own scoping decision and CSSE's own current self-description. It is included here because it is a form of the same underlying risk this finding is about: a fact that looks settled (which schools does CSSE mean by "its member schools") was found to be genuinely contested by a live official source, at the exact moment this programme needed it to be settled.

**A caveat on generalising the CCHSG/KEGS pattern:** both are Essex CC-administered schools; Southend-administered schools (SHSB, SHSG, WHSB, WHSG) were not observed to show the identical "PAN stable, criteria mechanics volatile" pattern — SHSG's PAN itself moved by 44 places, and WHSG's dispute is about the PAN, not the criteria mechanics beneath it. It would be an evidence-availability artefact (Readiness Report §10, Risk 4) to conclude Essex-administered schools are "more stable" than Southend-administered ones; the honest conclusion is narrower — volatility is real and material across both administering authorities, but it does not take an identical shape in each.

## 3. Implication for Angel

**Classification: `ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` is aligned in practice for what it currently proposes to build, but that is because it deliberately builds almost nothing on volatile data — it does not yet demonstrate that the underlying `KNOWLEDGE_GOVERNANCE.md` lifecycle model is sufficient for the volatility this evidence has now revealed.**

Reasoning:
- The Admissions Intelligence design's *only* concrete admissions fact it proposes to surface is CSSE's 303 combined-score floor (§3, §5, §9), justified in the design itself as "a disclosed, sourced first-pass value" analogous to other static constants already in code. Section 1.F above independently confirms this specific fact is in fact the single most stable, most multiply-corroborated figure in the entire Phase 2 corpus — so for *this one fact*, treating it as a durable constant is well-supported by the evidence, and the design's caution (never blending it into Readiness, always showing it "beside" real evidence, §5, §11) is sound regardless.
- However, the design explicitly does **not yet populate** the `school` / `school_admission_threshold` / `consortium_threshold_fact` schema it has already proposed (§9: "PROPOSED, empty schema only — do not populate without real data acquisition"). That schema is exactly where PAN, priority-area boundaries, and thresholds — the fields Section 1 shows changing most years and sometimes conflicting between official sources — would eventually live. The design has therefore not yet been tested against the reliability problem this finding documents, because it has not yet reached into the part of the data model where that problem lives.
- `KNOWLEDGE_GOVERNANCE.md` §7's lifecycle (Added → Reviewed → Updated → Superseded → Archived) is well-suited to the evidence type it was visibly designed around — exam papers and mark schemes, which change on an examination board's own multi-year syllabus cycle and rarely conflict with themselves. It has no explicit concept of:
  - a "this fact is only valid for admissions year N" tag distinct from "when was this document published" (Provenance Standard §5 has Publication Date and Date Added, but nothing that says "this PAN figure expires or must be re-checked before the next entry year");
  - a mandatory re-verification cadence — the lifecycle is triggered by a *new version becoming available* being noticed and added, not by a *scheduled check* that the currently-held figure is still current;
  - a formal mechanism for holding two Level-1-vs-Level-1 sources in permanent, visible disagreement rather than one superseding the other (§7's "Superseded" model assumes a newer asset cleanly replaces an older one — it does not describe what to do when two same-vintage official sources simply disagree, which is exactly the WHSG and SHSG situations in Section 1.D).

**Classification, stated plainly:** *Partially aligned.* The governance standard's core discipline (classify, provenance, cite, never invent) is exactly right and should not be touched. What it lacks is specific to the *admissions* evidence type, not a flaw in the general model — the same standard was never asked, until this evidence, to hold a fact that changes on an annual administrative cycle and can legitimately conflict between two contemporaneous official sources.

**Recommendation: strengthen — do not replace, retire, or hide.**

The 303-floor treatment already in the Admissions Intelligence design is a working example of the right instinct (disclosed, sourced, treated as durable only because the evidence supports durability) and should be the template. What "strengthen" would concretely mean, without building any of it now:
1. **A mandatory "evidence year" tag** on any admissions-specific fact (PAN, priority-area definition, threshold), distinct from the existing Publication Date/Date Added fields — recording which specific entry year the figure applies to, since this evidence shows the same school can legitimately have a different correct answer for consecutive entry years.
2. **An explicit "conflicting sources, unresolved" state** in the Review Status vocabulary (alongside "under review / accepted / superseded / archived") for exactly the WHSG- and SHSG-shaped situations — so a fact in this state is structurally prevented from being surfaced to a parent as a single confident number until a human resolves or the Founder accepts the ambiguity.
3. **An annual re-verification cadence** specific to admissions facts (not exam-paper facts) — a scheduled prompt to re-check any populated `school_admission_threshold` row against the current cycle's primary source before the next admissions season, rather than relying on someone noticing a new document exists.
4. **A visible "as of [year]" disclosure on any admissions figure shown to a parent** — the same "beside, never blended" discipline the design already applies to the 303 floor, extended to require a date-scoped caveat specifically because Section 1 shows these particular figures are not safe to treat as evergreen the way the 303 floor is.

None of this is proposed as a redesign of `KNOWLEDGE_GOVERNANCE.md` itself — it is a proposed *addition*, scoped to the admissions-data category specifically, leaving the exam-paper-evidence governance untouched.

## 4. Founder Review

Every recommendation in Section 3 is provisional and offered for evaluation only — none of it has been implemented, and no file governed by `KNOWLEDGE_GOVERNANCE.md` or `ADMISSIONS_INTELLIGENCE_V1_DESIGN.md` has been modified in producing this finding. The classification ("partially aligned") and the recommendation ("strengthen") are this synthesis's assessment of the evidence, not a decision — whether, when, and how to strengthen the governance standard for admissions data remains a Founder-approval-only architectural decision, consistent with the programme's standing hard gate between evidence synthesis and any implementation. This finding does not infer what the Founder will decide, and takes no position on priority or timing relative to the rest of the Assessment Excellence Programme's Phase 3 work.
