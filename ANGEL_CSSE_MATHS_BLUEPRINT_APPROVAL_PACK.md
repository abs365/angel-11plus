### mr05-bp-hcf-equal-groups

- **Competency / family / skill:** MR-05 / `mr05-factors-primes` / QT-MR-11  |  **Representation:** prose  |  **Prepared:** 14  |  **Difficulty:** medium 6, easy 8
- **Purpose:** Recognise that sharing two quantities into identical groups with nothing left over asks for their highest common factor, and find it.
- **Misconception targeted:** using the smaller number, or the lowest common multiple, when the situation asks for the highest common factor
- **Structural difference from existing material:** Existing HCF items ask 'What is the HCF of a and b?' with no situation. Here the child must first recognise that 'identical groups, nothing left over, as many as possible' means HCF. The computation is the same; the recognition step is new.
- **Transfer value:** High. It is the standard exam form of HCF. A child who only knows the bare procedure cannot answer it unprompted.
- **Deterministic validation (live run, 600 generated):** 600/600 passed range, constraint and answer re-derivation; 506/600 also passed the duplicate check (rejections: exact_duplicate_within_batch 94). Independent-oracle tests for this blueprint are in the test suite named in the pack footer.
- **Diversity check:** 1101 distinct questions in 4000 draws (MODERATE); 3 context tag(s) in the prepared set. Wording rotates over three situations only; the numbers are always g×m and g×n with coprime m, n, so the HCF is always the intended g (3 to 12).
- **Recommended publish volume:** 14 of 14
- **Representative samples:**
  - _medium band_ — A shop has 56 apples and 63 oranges. They are packed into identical baskets, each with the same number of apples and the same number of oranges, with no fruit left over. What is the greatest number of baskets that can be filled? → **7**. Every group has the same amount of each kind with none left over, so the number of groups must divide both 56 and 63 exactly. The greatest number that divides both is the highest common factor: HCF(56, 63) = 7.
  - _easy band_ — A teacher has 40 red counters and 48 blue counters. She makes identical groups, each with the same number of red counters and the same number of blue counters, and uses every counter. What is the greatest number of groups she can make? → **8**. Every group has the same amount of each kind with none left over, so the number of groups must divide both 40 and 48 exactly. The greatest number that divides both is the highest common factor: HCF(40, 48) = 8.

  - _edge: largest answer generated_ — A class has 24 boys and 108 girls. ### mr05-bp-lcm-repeating-events

- **Competency / family / skill:** MR-05 / `mr05-factors-primes` / QT-MR-11  |  **Representation:** prose  |  **Prepared:** 14  |  **Difficulty:** medium 7, easy 4, hard 3
- **Purpose:** Recognise that two repeating events coinciding again asks for the lowest common multiple, and find it.
- **Misconception targeted:** multiplying the two intervals, or finding a common factor, instead of the lowest common multiple
- **Structural difference from existing material:** Existing LCM items are bare 'LCM of a and b'. Here the situation (two repeating events) must be read as 'next time they coincide'. Pairs are chosen so neither interval divides the other, so the answer is never just the larger number.
- **Transfer value:** High. Recognising 'next time together' as LCM is the usual exam context.
- **Deterministic validation (live run, 600 generated):** 600/600 passed range, constraint and answer re-derivation; 458/600 also passed the duplicate check (rejections: exact_duplicate_within_batch 142). Independent-oracle tests for this blueprint are in the test suite named in the pack footer.
- **Diversity check:** 735 distinct questions in 4000 draws (MODERATE); 3 context tag(s) in the prepared set. Three situations only. Small number range (3 to 20) means a modest set of distinct pairs.
- **Recommended publish volume:** 14 of 14
- **Representative samples:**
  - _medium band_ — Asha visits the library every 6 days and Ben visits every 13 days. They are both there today. After how many days will they next both be there? → **78**. They coincide again at a time that is a multiple of both 6 and 13; the first such time is the lowest common multiple. HCF(6, 13) = 1, so LCM = (6 × 13) ÷ 1 = 78.
  - _easy band_ — Two lights start flashing at the same moment. One flashes every 18 seconds and the other every 4 seconds. After how many seconds will they next flash together? → **36**. They coincide again at a time that is a multiple of both 18 and 4; the first such time is the lowest common multiple. HCF(18, 4) = 2, so LCM = (18 × 4) ÷ 2 = 36.
  - _hard band_ — Asha visits the library every 15 days and Ben visits every 13 days. They are both there today. After how many days will they next both be there? → **195**. They coincide again at a time that is a multiple of both 15 and 13; the first such time is the lowest common multiple. HCF(15, 13) = 1, so LCM = (15 × 13) ÷ 1 = 195.
  - _edge: smallest answer generated_ — Asha visits the library every 6 days and Ben visits every 4 days. They are both there today. After how many days will they next both be there? → **12**. They coincide again at a time that is a multiple of both 6 and 4; the first such time is the lowest common multiple. HCF(6, 4) = 2, so LCM = (6 × 4) ÷ 2 = 12.
  - _edge: largest answer generated_ — Two lights start flashing at the same moment. One flashes every 15 seconds and the other every 16 seconds. After how many seconds will they next flash together? → **240**. They coincide again at a time that is a multiple of both 15 and 16; the first such time is the lowest common multiple.
