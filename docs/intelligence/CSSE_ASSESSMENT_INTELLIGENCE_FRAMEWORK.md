## 1. Purpose

This framework describes what the CSSE Knowledge Domain's Accepted evidence objectively demonstrates about the structure of the CSSE 11+ examination — its components, timing, marking approach, and content patterns. It exists to give Angel 11+ a factual, traceable, source-grounded description of the CSSE assessment to inform later, separately-authorised work (architecture, implementation). It is deliberately restricted to what the evidence shows, not what it might imply about how to teach to it, which questions to expect next, or how any individual learner should be assessed — those are different kinds of work, out of scope here (Section 9).

## 3. Assessment Overview

Per CSSE-001, candidates sit **two tests**: one English, one Mathematics, on the same day. Each paper's raw score is mathematically standardised and weighted, each worth 50% of the final combined score; results are age-adjusted by date of birth. There is no re-marking policy. No offer is made below a total standardised score of 303.

The English test is itself internally divided into three timed sections (Comprehension, Applied Reasoning, Continuous Writing) administered as a single 70-minute sitting (Section 4 below). The Mathematics test is a single 60-minute paper. CSSE-001 states its own free familiarisation papers are "not of the same format" as commercially available 11+ practice material — an explicit, direct claim about format distinctiveness, corroborated indirectly by the specific, non-generic Applied Reasoning task types observed in Section 4.

## 4. Assessment Components

### English

The English paper (CSSE-003/008/013) states on its own cover: *"This test consists of three parts: COMPREHENSION (30 minutes plus 10 minutes reading time), APPLIED REASONING QUESTIONS (10 minutes), CONTINUOUS WRITING (20 minutes). TOTAL TIME: 1 hour 10 minutes."* This is corroborated by CSSE-001's independent statement that "the English paper will last sixty minutes with ten minutes additional reading time" (60 + 10 = 70 minutes — consistent, cross-document).

- **Comprehension** (Section One): candidates read a passage (10 minutes reading time) then answer questions on it (30 minutes). All three available years use extracts from real, identifiable published narrative fiction: *A Traveller in Time* by Alison Uttley (2021, per CSSE-015), *The Good Companions* (2022, per CSSE-010), and *I Capture the Castle* by Dodie Smith (2023, per CSSE-005). Mark schemes explicitly instruct flexible acceptance of paraphrased or differently-worded correct answers (e.g. CSSE-005: "Accept any logical reason"; "One mark per clear answer. Direct quotations are fine as are generalised comments.").
- **Applied Reasoning** (Section Two): a fixed 10-minute, 5-question, 5-mark section in all three years, but the specific task mechanic differs every year — 2021 (CSSE-013): missing shared letter-pairs between two same-meaning words; 2022 (CSSE-008): missing shared letter-pairs, reversed order, between two words; 2023 (CSSE-003): alphabet-position numeric coding of a four-letter word. All three are letter/word-code puzzle types, not standard verbal-reasoning-bank formats (analogy, odd-one-out, etc.).
- **Continuous Writing** (Section Three): administered as a physically separate booklet ("Go to the separate booklet on your desk," CSSE-003/008/013) but within the same overall sitting — the Continuous Writing paper itself states it "may be completed at any time during the total 60 minutes" (referring to the three sections' combined time, excluding the 10-minute reading period), with "20 minutes" suggested. All three years require **two** prompts to be answered ("Answer BOTH questions in your own words"), with a minimum-length instruction of "at least six sentences." Topics vary each year with no evident recurring theme (2021: favourite building; 2022: food and mood; 2023: favourite place to relax).

### Mathematics

The Mathematics paper (CSSE-006/011/016) is a single 60-minute paper, 12 questions, totalling 60 marks in all three years (CSSE-007/012/017: "1 mark for each correct answer. Total 60 marks"), with electronic calculators explicitly prohibited in all three years. Marking is exact-match per final answer — no partial/method marks are described in any of the three mark schemes read. Question content spans multiple mathematical domains within a single paper: arithmetic calculation, algebraic/symbolic reasoning (solving for unknowns represented by symbols), geometric/spatial reasoning (identifying congruent 3D shapes), word problems (elapsed-time calculation), and number theory (properties of prime numbers) — observed directly in the 2023 paper (CSSE-006), consistent with the "Working out in the spaces on the paper" instruction present in all three years.

### Continuous Writing — marking approach

The only available official Continuous Writing marking guidance (CSSE-002) is a **4-band, 5-criterion qualitative rubric** (Ideas, Vocabulary [incl. spelling], Grammar, Structure, Punctuation), not a per-question numeric scheme. This sits in tension with the year-specific Continuous Writing papers themselves, which each print a numeric mark allocation for the section (15 marks in 2021 and 2022, 20 marks in 2023, per CSSE-014/009/004) — no Accepted asset explains how the 4-band rubric converts into that numeric total. This is recorded as an open evidence gap (Section 7), not resolved by inference.

Each entry below is a discrete observation with its evidentiary basis, confidence, and limitations.

- Evidence References: CSSE-001, "Your child will take two separate tests, one in English and one in Mathematics" / "each being worth 50% of the marks" / "minimum score... 303" / "joint policy to not re-mark test papers"
- Known Limitations: Single-asset evidence (Level B, administrative), not itself an examination paper. Not independently corroborated by a second official source in this evidence base.

- Known Limitations: None material — timing statements are consistent across all three years and cross-validated against the independent administrative guide.

It shows *that* a two-component scoring structure exists in the most recent year, not *how* the rubric produces it. Observed in one year only (2023); this framework does not assume the split existed informally in earlier years without being printed on the marker box, nor that it will persist in future years.

## 6. Evidence Confidence

Rating scale used throughout this framework, as specified by AEP-002:

- **HIGH** — directly and explicitly stated in one or more Accepted assets, consistent across all years where multiple years are available, no material limitation identified.
- **MEDIUM** — directly evidenced but based on incomplete coverage (e.g. one of three years unreadable, or only one year reviewed in depth), or evidenced with an acknowledged single-source/uncorroborated caveat.
- **LOW** — evidenced only as an unresolved gap or tension between sources, not as a positive finding; stated to preserve visibility of the gap, not to assert a conclusion.
- **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** — applied within an observation (not as a separate observation) wherever the evidence supports a structural pattern but cannot support a predictive or generalising claim beyond it (e.g. Observations 5, 6, 8).

## 7. Evidence Limitations

Carried forward unchanged from `KA-001_KNOWLEDGE_ACQUISITION_REPORT.md` and `KA-002_EVIDENCE_VERIFICATION_REPORT.md`, per AEP-002's explicit instruction:

- No official year-specific Continuous Writing mark schemes exist — only the one generic, undated sample (CSSE-002) is available, and Observation 10 documents the resulting gap between that rubric and the numeric totals on the actual papers.
- No official papers exist for entry years before 2021 in this evidence base.
- No published examiner moderation reports exist beyond the administrative Information Guide (CSSE-001) already acquired.

Additional limitations surfaced during this framework's own analysis, not previously documented:

- The 2022 English combined mark total (Comprehension + Applied Reasoning) could not be reliably extracted from CSSE-010 by this framework's text-extraction tooling (Observation 4) — a tooling gap in this specific analysis pass, not necessarily an absence of the information in the source PDF.
- This framework covers only three entry years (2021–2023). Every observation describing a "pattern" across years is a pattern across exactly three data points, and is rated accordingly (Section 6) — no claim in this framework should be read as covering CSSE's assessment design beyond what three consecutive years can show.

*Resolved under CAP-1.1 (2026-07-20):* Mathematics question-content analysis (Observation 11) was originally performed to full depth for only one of the three available years (2023); AEP-004's subsequent close reading extended full question-by-question review to all three years (see Correction Log). This limitation no longer applies and is retained here, struck through in effect, only for audit continuity.

## 9. Framework Boundaries

This framework does not:

- Predict future papers.
- Infer examiner intent.
- Recommend teaching strategies.
- Classify learner ability.
- Generate practice questions.

Every observation in Section 5 is restricted to describing what the Accepted evidence itself states or shows. Where an observation could have been extended into a prediction, recommendation, or classification, it was instead rated INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE or LOW and left there, per Section 6 — this boundary was treated as a hard stop during analysis, not a formatting note applied afterward.
