# CSSE Competency Framework

**Angel 11+, Version 3.0 Academic Excellence Programme**
**Work Package:** AEP-003 — CSSE Competency Framework
**Status:** Complete. Evidence-derived competency identification only — no teaching methods, no learner journeys, no adaptive learning, no application code.

---

## 1. Purpose

This framework identifies the knowledge, skills and reasoning competencies that the CSSE assessment, as described in `CSSE_ASSESSMENT_INTELLIGENCE_FRAMEWORK.md` (AEP-002), objectively requires a learner to demonstrate. It exists to translate AEP-002's structural observations about the examination into a named, organised catalogue of competencies suitable to inform later, separately-authorised architecture work. It does not describe how any competency should be taught, practised, or measured in a learner-facing product — those are different kinds of work, explicitly out of scope here (Section 8).

## 2. Evidence Base

This framework uses exclusively:

- `docs/intelligence/CSSE_ASSESSMENT_INTELLIGENCE_FRAMEWORK.md` (AEP-002) — all 12 observations and their confidence ratings.
- The 17 Accepted CSSE Knowledge Assets it is built on (CSSE-001 through CSSE-017), cited directly where a competency draws on specific evidence detail present in an asset but not spelled out as its own numbered observation in AEP-002 (e.g. the exact wording of a mark scheme's acceptable answers).

No external educational source, commercial tutoring material, or AI-generated competency taxonomy was used. Where AEP-002 itself flagged a limitation (INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, a single-year confirmation, an unresolved gap), that limitation is carried into this framework rather than smoothed over by naming a broader competency than the evidence supports.

## 3. Competency Principles

1. **A competency is named only where the assessment evidence shows a learner must actually do something specific** — answer a particular kind of question, produce a particular kind of output, apply a particular kind of reasoning — not because a plausible-sounding skill could be associated with a subject area in general.
2. **Competencies are described at the level of abstraction the evidence actually supports, not the most specific or most general level available.** Where a structural pattern is stable but its concrete instantiation varies (e.g. Applied Reasoning's genre versus its yearly mechanic — AEP-002 Observation 5), the competency is named at the stable level, and the instability is recorded as a limitation rather than papered over or used to justify a narrower, less defensible claim.
3. **No domain or competency is invented to complete a tidy-looking taxonomy.** AEP-003's own instruction lists possible domains as examples only; a domain (e.g. "Vocabulary") is created here only where evidence distinct from an existing domain supports it — see Section 4's explicit note on why Vocabulary was not created as a standalone domain.
4. **Every competency carries an Evidence Maturity Classification (EMC)**, defined as follows for this framework (not previously defined elsewhere in this repository, since AEP-003 introduces the term without a prior definition to reuse):

   - **EMC-4 (Established):** Directly and consistently evidenced across all years available for the relevant observation, drawn from an AEP-002 observation rated HIGH confidence, with no material limitation affecting the competency claim itself.
   - **EMC-3 (Substantiated):** Directly evidenced, but drawn from a MEDIUM-confidence AEP-002 observation, or from a HIGH-confidence observation whose own stated limitation is partial-year coverage rather than an inconsistency in what is shown.
   - **EMC-2 (Indicative):** Evidenced only in a single year, or only as one example within a broader pattern AEP-002 itself could not generalise beyond three data points.
   - **EMC-1 (Provisional):** The competency can be named from the evidence, but its precise boundaries or scoring treatment are left genuinely unresolved by an explicit gap in the evidence (e.g. AEP-002 Observation 10).

   EMC is a statement about how mature the *evidence for this competency's existence and shape* is — it is not a statement about how important, difficult, or teachable the competency is, none of which this framework addresses.
5. **Relationships between competencies are recorded only where the evidence shows a structural fact** (same assessment section, same paper, same timing, same scoring weight) — never a causal, developmental, or prerequisite claim, which no asset in this evidence base could support (Section 6).

## 4. Competency Domains

Four domains are supported by the evidence, corresponding to the assessment components AEP-002 identified (§4 of that framework):

- **Reading Comprehension (RC)** — evidenced by AEP-002 Observations 2, 6, 7 and the underlying Comprehension mark schemes (CSSE-005, CSSE-010, CSSE-015).
- **Applied Reasoning (AR)** — evidenced by AEP-002 Observation 5 and the underlying Applied Reasoning question papers (CSSE-003, CSSE-008, CSSE-013).
- **Mathematical Reasoning (MR)** — evidenced by AEP-002 Observations 3 and 11 and the underlying Mathematics papers (CSSE-006, CSSE-011, CSSE-016) and mark schemes (CSSE-007, CSSE-012, CSSE-017).
- **Written Communication (WC)** — evidenced by AEP-002 Observations 8, 9 and 10 and the underlying Continuous Writing papers (CSSE-004, CSSE-009, CSSE-014) and rubric (CSSE-002).

**Vocabulary was considered and deliberately not created as a standalone domain.** The only direct evidence of a vocabulary-specific demand is a single Comprehension mark scheme entry (2023, CSSE-005: a question accepting "to relax/to lay around lazily/luxuriously in warmth" as the required meaning of a word used in the passage) — one instance, in one year, embedded within a Comprehension question rather than presented as its own section or paper. This is recorded as Reading Comprehension competency RC-03 (Section 5) rather than a separate domain, since a single instance within an existing domain does not meet Principle 3's bar for inventing a new domain. Continuous Writing's rubric separately assesses "Vocabulary (incl. spelling)" as one of five criteria (CSSE-002) — this is captured within WC-02, not as a cross-cutting Vocabulary domain, since it is evidenced only as one dimension of a writing-quality rubric, not as an independently assessed skill.

## 5. Competency Catalogue

---

### RC-01 — Literal Retrieval from Narrative Text

- **Description:** Locating and reproducing (by quotation or close paraphrase) specific, explicitly-stated information from a narrative passage.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 6 (passages are narrative fiction, 3/3 years); Observation 7 (mark schemes accept direct quotation or generalised comment for full marks).
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-005, CSSE-010, CSSE-015 (mark schemes); CSSE-003, CSSE-008, CSSE-013 (question papers).
- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-3 — directly evidenced (e.g. CSSE-005 Q1: "It is fine to accept a direct quotation... Need at least 'her paws scrape.'"), but confirmed within narrative fiction only (Observation 6's own genre-coverage limitation carries forward).
- **Known Limitations:** Only demonstrated against narrative fiction texts in this evidence base; whether the same retrieval demand appears against non-fiction or poetry cannot be established (no such text is present in the evidence base).

---

### RC-02 — Inference and Justified Interpretation

- **Description:** Drawing a conclusion not explicitly stated in the text and supporting it with a reasoned explanation, not merely a located quotation.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 7 (flexible acceptance of reasoned answers).
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-005 (2023, e.g. Q2b: "Give one mark for a reasonable interpretation... Accept any logical reason"), CSSE-015 (2021, e.g. Q12b: "Explain two ways in which [the author] has the mother behave that supports your answer" — 4 marks).
- **Confidence Rating:** MEDIUM
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-3 — directly evidenced in two of the three available years (2021, 2023); the 2022 mark scheme (CSSE-010) was not reviewed to this level of question-by-question detail in AEP-002, so this competency's presence in 2022 is unconfirmed, not contradicted.
- **Known Limitations:** Not cross-verified against the 2022 paper. Distinguishing "inference" from "retrieval" (RC-01) in borderline cases relies on this framework's own reading of the mark schemes' language, not on CSSE labelling questions by type.

---

### RC-03 — Word/Phrase Meaning-in-Context Explanation

- **Description:** Explaining the meaning of a specific word or phrase as used within the passage.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** Not separately numbered in AEP-002; drawn directly from the same underlying evidence as Observation 7.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-005 (2023 only — Q4: "Accept: to relax/to lay around lazily/luxuriously in warmth. Must have an aspect of enjoying warmth to relax and feel lazy.").
- **Confidence Rating:** LOW
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-2 — a single instance, in a single year, is the entire evidentiary basis for this competency.
- **Known Limitations:** Observed once. Cannot be confirmed as a recurring feature of the Comprehension section rather than an incidental question in one year's paper.

---

### RC-04 — Sequential Ordering of Textual Information

- **Description:** Identifying and correctly ordering a sequence of actions or events described in the passage.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** Not separately numbered in AEP-002; drawn directly from the same underlying evidence as Observation 7.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-005 (2023 only — Q3: ordering three actions correctly, with explicit partial credit for partially-correct ordering: "if they write 'get in bath/wash/read' they get two marks... if they write 'bask/read/wash' they get one mark").
- **Confidence Rating:** LOW
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-2 — single instance, single year.
- **Known Limitations:** Same as RC-03 — observed once, not confirmed as recurring.

---

### AR-01 — Letter-Code Pattern Inference and Application

- **Description:** Inferring an unstated rule governing a letter-based code or pattern from worked examples, then applying that inferred rule to solve new instances.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 5.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-003 (2023 — alphabet-position numeric coding), CSSE-008 (2022 — shared letter-pairs, reversed order between two words), CSSE-013 (2021 — shared letter-pairs, same meaning between two words).
- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH for the abstract competency (rule-inference-and-application from a letter/word code); INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE for any specific mechanic.
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-3 at the abstract level defined above. The concrete mechanic a learner would need to apply in any future year is EMC-1 — three different mechanics have been observed in three years, with no repeat.
- **Known Limitations:** This is the clearest case in this framework of a competency that must be described at a higher level of abstraction than the concrete task, per Principle 2 — naming this competency as "solve [2023's specific cipher type]" would overstate what three non-repeating years can support.

---

### MR-01 — Arithmetic Calculation

- **Description:** Performing multi-step arithmetic calculations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, decimals, percentages) without a calculator.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 3 (no calculator, 3/3 years); Observation 11 (arithmetic content directly observed).
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-006, CSSE-007, CSSE-011, CSSE-012, CSSE-016, CSSE-017.
- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH for the no-calculator condition; MEDIUM for the specific arithmetic content depth.
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-3 — the no-calculator condition is EMC-4 quality evidence in isolation, but Observation 11's own limitation (full question-content depth reviewed for 2023 only) caps this competency's rating.
- **Known Limitations:** Content-level confirmation is for 2023 (CSSE-006) only; 2021/2022 papers were confirmed for timing/marks/calculator policy but not reviewed question-by-question in AEP-002.

---

### MR-02 — Algebraic / Symbolic Problem-Solving

- **Description:** Solving for unknown values represented by symbols or shapes, using given numeric relationships between them.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 11.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-006 (2023 only — Q8: solving for the value of a repeated symbol across three sum equations).
- **Confidence Rating:** LOW
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-2 — single year, single instance reviewed.
- **Known Limitations:** Not confirmed in 2021 or 2022 papers, which were not reviewed to question-level depth.

---

### MR-03 — Geometric and Spatial Reasoning

- **Description:** Identifying, comparing, and reasoning about the properties of shapes, including recognising congruent or identical forms presented in different orientations.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 11.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-006 (2023 only — Q7: identifying pairs of identical shapes formed from four glued cubes).
- **Confidence Rating:** LOW
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-2 — single year, single instance reviewed.
- **Known Limitations:** Same as MR-02.

---

### MR-04 — Multi-Step Word-Problem Interpretation

- **Description:** Translating a real-world scenario described in prose into a mathematical calculation, including problems requiring more than one step (e.g. elapsed time across multiple repeated events).
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 11.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-006 (2023 only — Q9: calculating finish/start times for three sequential 1hr 37min tasks).
- **Confidence Rating:** LOW
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-2 — single year, single instance reviewed.
- **Known Limitations:** Same as MR-02/MR-03.

---

### MR-05 — Number Properties and Number Theory

- **Description:** Reasoning about properties of numbers (e.g. prime numbers) beyond direct calculation, including finding numbers satisfying a stated numeric property.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 11.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-006 (2023 only — Q10: finding pairs of prime numbers with a stated difference).
- **Confidence Rating:** LOW
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-2 — single year, single instance reviewed.
- **Known Limitations:** Same as MR-02/MR-03/MR-04.

---

### MR-06 — Precision Under Exact-Match, No-Partial-Credit Conditions

- **Description:** Producing a fully correct final answer, since Mathematics marking awards one mark per correct final answer with no method or partial credit described. This is a cross-cutting demand on how MR-01 through MR-05 must be demonstrated, not a content domain of its own.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 3.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-007, CSSE-012, CSSE-017 ("1 mark for each correct answer" — identical wording, 3/3 years).
- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-4 — consistently and identically evidenced across all three available years, with no material limitation.
- **Known Limitations:** None material. This describes the marking condition as documented; it does not establish whether any working-out credit exists informally in practice, which no asset in this evidence base could show.

---

### WC-01 — Sustained Original Composition Under Time/Length Constraint

- **Description:** Producing original, extended written responses to two given prompts within a fixed time allowance and a stated minimum length.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 8.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-004, CSSE-009, CSSE-014 ("Answer BOTH questions in your own words" / "at least six sentences," consistent 3/3 years).
- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-3 — the structural format is consistently evidenced (3/3 years), capped below EMC-4 because Observation 8 itself notes prompt topics vary with no evident pattern, meaning the full shape of what a learner must be ready to write about is not established.
- **Known Limitations:** Topic domain is not predictable from this evidence base (Observation 8).

---

### WC-02 — Multi-Dimensional Writing Quality (Ideas, Vocabulary, Grammar, Structure, Punctuation)

- **Description:** Producing writing that satisfies five distinct quality dimensions identified in CSSE's own rubric: idea development/originality, vocabulary range and spelling accuracy, grammatical control, structural organisation (including paragraphing), and punctuation accuracy.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Observation 10.
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-002 (the 4-band, 5-criterion rubric).
- **Confidence Rating:** LOW — the rubric's existence and content is well-evidenced, but its relationship to the numeric marks actually printed on each year's paper (Observation 9) is not established by any Accepted asset.
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-1 — this is the framework's clearest Provisional case: the five quality dimensions are real (directly stated in CSSE-002), but Observation 10's unresolved rubric-versus-numeric-total gap means this competency's actual scoring boundaries cannot be stated with confidence.
- **Known Limitations:** CSSE-002 is undated and generic (not year-specific — Asset Profile CSSE-002); no year-specific Continuous Writing mark scheme exists in this evidence base to confirm the rubric was applied unchanged in 2021, 2022, or 2023.

## 6. Competency Relationships

Only structural relationships evidenced by the assessment's own organisation are recorded here. No causal, developmental, or prerequisite relationship is asserted between any competencies — no asset in this evidence base could support such a claim.

- **RC-01 through RC-04 co-occur within the same assessment section** (English Section One, Comprehension) and are drawn from the same passage in the same sitting (AEP-002 Observation 2). This is a co-location fact, not a claim that one enables another.
- **AR-01 is administered as a separate, separately-timed section (Section Two) within the same English paper as the RC competencies**, immediately following Comprehension and before Continuous Writing (AEP-002 Observation 2). No evidence in this base relates AR-01's demands to RC-01–RC-04's.
- **WC-01 and WC-02 are administered in a physically separate booklet (Section Three) within the same overall English sitting** as the RC and AR competencies, though completable "at any time during the total 60 minutes" per the Continuous Writing paper's own instruction (AEP-002 Observation 2). This is a scheduling/administration relationship, not an evidenced content relationship.
- **MR-01 through MR-06 belong to an entirely separate paper (Mathematics) from all English-paper competencies**, sat on the same day but scored, weighted, and standardised independently (AEP-002 Observation 1: each paper worth 50% of the combined standardised score). No asset in this evidence base relates performance on any Mathematics competency to any English-paper competency.
- **MR-06 (precision under exact-match conditions) applies across MR-01–MR-05** as a scoring condition rather than a sixth content domain — recorded in MR-06's own description above, not as a separate relationship claim beyond what that description already states.

## 8. Framework Boundaries

This framework does not:

- Prescribe teaching methods.
- Recommend revision strategies.
- Estimate learner ability.
- Generate practice questions.
- Replace professional educational judgement.

Every competency above describes a demand the assessment evidence shows exists — it says nothing about how a learner should be taught to meet that demand, how much practice is needed, or how any individual learner is currently performing against it. Where a competency's evidence was strong enough that a narrower, more actionable-sounding claim could have been made, the broader, better-supported framing was kept instead (Principle 2, Section 3) — this boundary was applied during identification, not as a disclaimer added afterward.

## 9. Readiness Assessment

This Competency Framework is **sufficient to support AEP-004**, subject to the following conditions carried forward from the Evidence Maturity Classifications in Section 5:

- Any AEP-004 work treating an EMC-1 or EMC-2 competency (RC-03, RC-04, AR-01's concrete mechanic, MR-02–MR-05, WC-02) as equivalently well-established to an EMC-3/EMC-4 competency would misrepresent this framework's own findings. AEP-004 should treat these as real but narrowly-evidenced, not as gaps to be filled by assumption.
- WC-02 in particular carries AEP-002's unresolved rubric-versus-numeric-total gap forward unchanged (Observation 10) — any AEP-004 work on Continuous Writing must treat this as an open question, not a solved one.
- Five of the twelve competencies (RC-03, RC-04, MR-02, MR-03, MR-04, MR-05 — six, in fact) rest on a single observed instance each. This is an honest reflection of the underlying evidence base's size (three entry years), not a defect introduced by this framework, but AEP-004 should not treat single-instance competencies as having the same breadth of support as the multi-year, cross-validated ones (MR-01, MR-06, WC-01, AR-01's abstract form, RC-01).

Within those conditions, every competency is evidence-based, traceable to specific observations and Asset IDs, confidence-rated, EMC-classified, and limitation-documented, per AEP-003's quality standards (all four criteria satisfied for all twelve competencies, verified in Section 7's traceability matrix).
