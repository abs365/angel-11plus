# CSSE Question Intelligence Framework

**Angel 11+, Version 3.0 Academic Excellence Programme**
**Work Package:** AEP-004 — CSSE Question Intelligence Framework
**Status:** Complete. Evidence-based question-level classification only — no learner journeys, no adaptive learning, no mock questions, no application code.

---

## 1. Purpose

This framework identifies what each official CSSE question, as described by the CSSE Assessment Intelligence Framework (AEP-002) and the CSSE Competency Framework (AEP-003), is objectively measuring. It exists to translate AEP-003's competency catalogue into a named, traceable classification of recurring *question types* — the concrete formats through which those competencies are actually assessed — suitable to inform later, separately-authorised work. It does not predict what future questions will look like, infer why CSSE chose any given format, recommend how to teach or practise toward it, or estimate how any learner would perform against it — those are different kinds of work, explicitly out of scope here (Section 8).

## 2. Evidence Base

This framework uses exclusively:

- `docs/intelligence/CSSE_ASSESSMENT_INTELLIGENCE_FRAMEWORK.md` (AEP-002) — all 12 observations and their confidence ratings.
- `docs/intelligence/CSSE_COMPETENCY_FRAMEWORK.md` (AEP-003) — all 13 competencies, their EMC ratings, and the EMC-1→4 scale it defines (reused here unchanged, per Principle 2 below).
- All 17 Accepted CSSE Knowledge Assets (CSSE-001 through CSSE-017), read directly at question level.

No external educational source, commercial tutoring material, or AI-generated question taxonomy was used.

**Verification method.** Identifying question *types* (as distinct from AEP-002's structural observations or AEP-003's competencies) requires reading every question in every paper, not a representative sample. All 17 asset PDFs were extracted to text (`pdftotext -layout`) and read directly against the original PDFs' question numbering, in the same manner AEP-002 used for its own close reading. This went further than AEP-002's own stated depth in one respect AEP-002 itself flagged as a limitation: AEP-002 §7 records that "Mathematics question-content analysis was performed to full depth for only one of the three available years (2023)." This framework read all three Mathematics papers (CSSE-006, CSSE-011, CSSE-016) and all three Mark Schemes (CSSE-007, CSSE-012, CSSE-017) to full question-by-question depth, because a question-type catalogue cannot be built from one year's paper alone without materially understating multi-year recurrence.

**New evidence surfaced by this closer reading, not present in AEP-002 or AEP-003, reported transparently per Principle 4 below — neither of the source frameworks was altered:**

1. **AEP-002 Observation 3's "12-question paper" figure does not match the source documents.** Each Mathematics paper's own "END OF TEST" line states the true count directly: CSSE-006 (2023) — "You should have completed 20 questions"; CSSE-011 (2022) and CSSE-016 (2021) — "You should have completed 21 questions." All three papers are internally numbered 1 through 20/21, most with lettered sub-parts (e.g. Q1(a)(b)(c)), consistent with the 60-mark total AEP-002 correctly identified. This is a factual discrepancy in AEP-002, discovered while establishing the primary-source basis this framework needed for its own Question Classification Model (Section 4). It is not corrected here — that would exceed AEP-004's authorised scope (Section 8) — but it materially affects any future reading of AEP-002 Observation 3, so it is recorded here and carried into the Readiness Assessment (Section 9) as a recommended correction work package.
2. **The 2023 Continuous Writing paper (CSSE-004) shows an explicit Content/SPAG marks split not present in the 2021 or 2022 papers.** CSSE-004's marker scoring box states "Content Max 15 marks / SPAG Max 5 marks" against its 20-mark total. CSSE-014 (2021) and CSSE-009 (2022) both show only a blank "1st marker's score / 2nd marker's score" box against their 15-mark totals, with no named sub-components. This is new evidence bearing on AEP-002 Observation 10's unresolved rubric-vs-numeric-marks gap: it shows *that* CSSE introduced an explicit two-component split in 2023 (coinciding with the 15→20 mark increase AEP-002 Observation 9 already noted), but it does not show how CSSE-002's 5-criterion rubric (Ideas / Vocabulary / Grammar / Structure / Punctuation) maps onto the two components — that mapping remains unstated in any Accepted asset. Observation 10's gap is therefore **partially narrowed, not resolved**, and is carried forward as still-open in Section 5 (WC-02-related entries) and Section 9.
3. **The two Continuous Writing prompts follow a stable, previously-unrecorded format pattern across all three years.** AEP-002 Observation 8 recorded that prompt *topics* vary with no evident theme — true, and unchanged here. This framework additionally found that prompt *format* is stable: Question 1 is always a reflective/discursive prompt answered from the candidate's own experience or opinion, and Question 2 is always "Write a story based on the picture below" (CSSE-004, CSSE-009, CSSE-014, identical instruction in all three years, each with an accompanying photograph). This is a genuine, evidence-supported, 3/3-year Question Type distinction (Section 5, QT-WC-01a/b) that neither AEP-002 nor AEP-003 named, because both worked one level of abstraction above individual question format.

## 3. Question Intelligence Principles

1. **A Question Type is named only where a recurring question *format* — not merely a topic or a competency — is directly observed in the evidence**, with year-recurrence (1/3, 2/3, or 3/3) recorded honestly as part of its Confidence Rating and EMC, never smoothed into a stronger claim than the evidence supports.
2. **Question Types are a finer grain than AEP-003's competencies, not a replacement for them.** Every Question Type maps to one Primary Competency (and, where evidenced, Supporting Competencies) already named in AEP-003; this framework does not invent, rename, split, or merge any AEP-003 competency. Several Question Types may legitimately share one Primary Competency — that is expected, since a competency can be assessed through more than one format.
3. **Measurement Purpose statements describe only what the format observably requires a candidate to produce or demonstrate** (e.g. "locate and reproduce an explicitly-stated fact") — never why CSSE includes it, never how it should be taught or practised, never how any learner would be expected to perform against it.
4. **Where this framework's own necessarily-close, question-level reading of the Accepted assets surfaces evidence beyond what AEP-002 or AEP-003 captured**, that evidence is reported transparently as new to this framework (Section 2 above) — never silently substituted for AEP-002/003's own findings, and never used to edit either document.
5. **No Question Type is invented to make the four Assessment Components look symmetrical.** Applied Reasoning yields one Question Type across all three years because the evidence shows one stable format (a letter/word-code puzzle) with a varying mechanic; Mathematics yields many more because the evidence shows many stable, recurring formats. This asymmetry is a finding, not a defect to be corrected by naming additional types the evidence does not support.
6. **Relationships between Question Types (Section 6) are recorded only as structural or co-location facts** — same section, same numbered question, same sitting, stable ordering — never as a causal, developmental, sequencing-by-difficulty, or prerequisite claim, matching the boundary rule already established by AEP-002 §9 and AEP-003 §3(5).

## 4. Question Classification Model

Each Question Type below is defined by the intersection of three axes, all directly observable in the evidence:

- **Assessment Component** — which of AEP-002's four described components the question belongs to (English Comprehension, English Applied Reasoning, English Continuous Writing, Mathematics).
- **Response Format** — the concrete shape of what a candidate must produce: a tick-box judgement, a short open answer, a bounded list, an ordered sequence, a quotation-plus-explanation pair, a coded-letter answer, a numeric/algebraic answer, an extended free-text composition, etc.
- **Reasoning Demand** — the kind of cognitive operation the format requires: literal retrieval, inference/justification, transformation or rule-application, computation, or generative composition.

A Question Type is only catalogued where a specific combination of these three axes recurs as an identifiable, repeated pattern within the evidence base — a one-off phrasing of a single question is not, by itself, a Question Type (Principle 1). Only evidence-supported categories are listed; several plausible formats that did **not** recur (e.g. a "match two columns" format, multiple-choice-from-four-options in Comprehension) were considered and excluded because no second instance was found.

**QT-AR-01 — Letter/Word-Code Rule Application**
- **Assessment Component:** English Applied Reasoning
- **Primary Competency:** AR-01 (Letter-Code Pattern Inference and Application)
- **Supporting Competencies:** —
- **Measurement Purpose:** Requires the candidate to infer an unstated rule linking a pair of words via letter positions or shared letter-pairs, from worked examples, then apply that inferred rule to encode, decode, or complete a partially-given code across five sub-questions per year.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Obs. 5
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-003 (2023 — alphabet-position numeric coding); CSSE-008 (2022 — shared letter-pairs, reversed); CSSE-013 (2021 — shared letter-pairs, same meaning)
- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH for the abstract format (5 questions, worked example, rule-then-apply structure — identical 3/3 years); INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE for the specific mechanic in any future year.
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-3 at the abstract level; EMC-1 for the concrete mechanic (three different mechanics observed, no repeat) — identical split to AEP-003's AR-01.
- **Known Limitations:** Within the five sub-questions each year, this framework observed a consistent internal micro-pattern (full-decode, full-encode, complete-one-missing-digit, complete-one-missing-digit-variant, find-missing-first-letter/digit) recurring in the same relative order across all three years' Applied Reasoning sections — this sub-structure was not separately named as its own Question Type because it is a fixed internal arrangement of one already-named type, not a distinct format in its own right (Principle 5).

### English Continuous Writing (Section Three)

---

**QT-WC-01a — Reflective/Discursive Response Prompt**
- **Assessment Component:** English Continuous Writing
- **Primary Competency:** WC-01 (Sustained Original Composition Under Time/Length Constraint)
- **Supporting Competencies:** WC-02
- **Measurement Purpose:** Requires the candidate to produce extended original writing (minimum six sentences) responding to a question or statement about their own experience, opinion, or imagination, always administered as Question 1.
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Obs. 8; new sub-classification per Section 2, finding 3
- **Supporting Asset IDs:** CSSE-004 Q1 (2023, "favourite place... to relax"); CSSE-009 Q1 (2022, "Do you think that food can change a person's mood?"); CSSE-014 Q1 (2021, favourite building)
- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-3 — format position (always Question 1) and structural demand (reflective/discursive, no stimulus image) consistent 3/3 years; capped below EMC-4 because, as AEP-002 Obs. 8 notes, topic content itself is unpredictable, so the full shape of what a candidate must be ready to write about is not established.
- **Known Limitations:** Same topic-unpredictability limitation as WC-01 in AEP-003.

**QT-WC-01b — Picture-Stimulus Narrative Prompt**
- **Assessment Component:** English Continuous Writing
- **Primary Competency:** WC-01 (Sustained Original Composition Under Time/Length Constraint)
- **Supporting Competencies:** WC-02
- **Measurement Purpose:** Requires the candidate to produce an original narrative (minimum six sentences) based on an accompanying photograph, always administered as Question 2, always using the identical instruction "Write a story based on the picture below."
- **Supporting Assessment Observation(s):** AEP-002 Obs. 8; new sub-classification per Section 2, finding 3

- **Confidence Rating:** HIGH
- **Evidence Maturity Classification:** EMC-4 — identical instruction wording and fixed Question-2 position confirmed directly in all three years, no material limitation on the format claim itself (unlike QT-WC-01a, the instruction text itself, not just the format shape, is stable).
- **Known Limitations:** The specific photograph changes each year (expected, and not itself evidence of anything beyond normal paper variation); this framework does not attempt to characterise what kind of image CSSE tends to choose, since only three images have been observed and no accepted asset describes a selection criterion.

## 6. Question Relationships

Only structural or co-location facts are recorded here, per Principle 6. No causal, developmental, sequencing-by-difficulty, or prerequisite relationship is asserted.

- **All ten Reading Comprehension Question Types (QT-RC-01 through QT-RC-10) co-occur within English Section One**, drawn from the same passage in the same sitting (AEP-002 Obs. 2).
- **Several CSSE Comprehension questions stack two Question Types within one numbered question's sub-parts** — e.g. CSSE-003 Q2 pairs QT-RC-02 (a: Yes/No) with a written-reasons continuation; CSSE-003 Q6 and Q7 similarly pair a judgement sub-part with an explanation/quotation sub-part. This is a real, observed feature of how CSSE constructs individual Comprehension questions, not a claim about which type is more fundamental.
- **QT-AR-01 is administered as a separately-timed section (Section Two)**, immediately after Comprehension and before Continuous Writing (AEP-002 Obs. 2). No evidence in this base relates AR-01's demands to any Comprehension Question Type's.
- **QT-WC-01a always precedes QT-WC-01b**: Question 1 is the reflective/discursive prompt and Question 2 is the picture-stimulus narrative prompt, in that fixed order, in all three years observed (Section 2, finding 3). This is a stable ordering fact; it is not evidence that one is easier, more foundational, or a prerequisite for the other.
- **Several Mathematics questions combine more than one MR Question Type within a single numbered question's sub-parts** — e.g. CSSE-006 Q14 combines QT-MR-03 (rounding/measurement) with QT-MR-07 (geometric perimeter/area); CSSE-006 Q9 combines QT-MR-10 (elapsed time) with QT-MR-01 (arithmetic). This shows CSSE Mathematics questions are frequently compound rather than single-skill — a structural fact about question construction, not a claim about relative difficulty.
- **QT-MR-14 (Precision Under Exact-Match Conditions) applies across every other Mathematics Question Type** as a scoring condition, not as a separate content relationship (mirrors AEP-003's treatment of MR-06).
- **All Mathematics Question Types belong to a wholly separate paper from every English-paper Question Type**, sat on the same day but scored, weighted, and standardised independently (AEP-002 Obs. 1). No asset in this evidence base relates performance on any Mathematics Question Type to any English-paper Question Type.

## 8. Framework Boundaries

This framework does not:

- Predict future questions.
- Infer examiner intent.
- Recommend teaching methods.
- Generate practice questions.
- Estimate learner performance.

Every Question Type above describes a format the evidence shows CSSE has used — it says nothing about whether that format will recur, why CSSE chose it, how a learner should prepare for it, or how any individual learner would perform against it. Where this framework's own closer reading found evidence AEP-002 or AEP-003 did not capture (Section 2), that evidence was reported transparently and used only to build this framework's own catalogue — never to edit either source document, which remains outside AEP-004's authorised scope.

## 9. Readiness Assessment

This Question Intelligence Framework is **sufficient to support AEP-005**, subject to the following conditions:

1. **A correction work package for AEP-002 Observation 3 is recommended before AEP-005 relies on it.** This framework independently verified, directly from CSSE-006/011/016's own "END OF TEST" statements, that the Mathematics paper contains 20 (2023) or 21 (2021, 2022) numbered questions, not 12 as AEP-002 Observation 3 currently states. This does not affect AEP-002's 60-minute/60-mark/no-calculator/exact-match findings, which remain independently well-evidenced, but the question-count figure specifically should not be treated as reliable until corrected.
2. **AEP-002 Observation 10's rubric-vs-numeric-marks gap remains open**, though partially narrowed: this framework found CSSE-004 (2023) carries an explicit Content(15)/SPAG(5) marks split absent from CSSE-009/CSSE-014 (2021/2022), which shows *that* a two-component structure exists in the most recent year but not *how* CSSE-002's five-criterion rubric maps onto it. Any AEP-005 work touching Continuous Writing scoring should treat this as still unresolved, not solved by this new evidence.
3. **Three Mathematics competencies' EMC ratings in AEP-003 (MR-02, MR-03, MR-04) were assigned EMC-2 on the basis of AEP-002's then-single-year (2023-only) depth review.** This framework's fuller three-year reading found direct, consistent multi-year evidence for all three (QT-MR-05/06 for MR-02, QT-MR-07/08 for MR-03, QT-MR-04/10/13 for MR-04). This framework does not alter AEP-003's own ratings — that is outside AEP-004's scope — but recommends a dedicated AEP-003 EMC revisit as a prerequisite, or early item, of AEP-005, since AEP-005 would otherwise inherit an understatement of how well-evidenced these three competencies actually are.
4. **Two recurring Mathematics demands — unit/measurement conversion (QT-MR-03) and data/chart/table reading (QT-MR-09) — have no dedicated AEP-003 competency**, and were mapped here to the closest existing competency (MR-01) with a documented imperfect fit. This framework does not invent a new competency to cover them (Principle 5; AEP-003 Principle 3's same discipline). Flagged for a future AEP-003 review, not resolved here.
5. **Any AEP-005 work should not treat EMC-1/EMC-2 Question Types (QT-RC-03, QT-RC-06, QT-RC-09, QT-MR-02, QT-MR-11's search sub-format) as equivalently established to the EMC-4 types** — they are real but single- or double-instance findings, an honest reflection of the underlying three-year evidence base's size, not a defect introduced by this framework.

Within those conditions, every Question Type above is evidence-based, traceable to specific Observations, a Primary Competency, and specific Asset IDs, confidence-rated, EMC-classified, and limitation-documented, per AEP-004's Quality Standards (all criteria satisfied for all 27 Question Types, verified in Section 7's traceability matrix).
