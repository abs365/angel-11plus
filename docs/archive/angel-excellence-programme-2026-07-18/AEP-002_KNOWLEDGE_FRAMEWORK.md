## Educational Outcome

*(Required section per AEP-001 §8 — every future educational document must explain how it improves understanding, confidence, examination performance, and long-term learning.)*

**Understanding:** before this document, Angel's knowledge structure was scattered across six separate files, three domains (Non-Verbal Reasoning, Spatial Reasoning, Numerical Reasoning) had no competency taxonomy at all despite having real, substantial content (119 questions combined), and nothing anywhere connected "what Angel teaches" to "what a specific exam board actually requires." This document gives every future content, product, and engineering decision one map to check against instead of re-deriving the picture each time.

**Confidence:** by making explicit which domains matter for which pathway (§6), this document lets Angel eventually tell a family precisely what their child needs — not a generic "practise everything" instruction, which itself undermines a parent's confidence that the product understands their specific situation (`ANGEL_EXPERIENCE_MANIFESTO.md`'s Parent Confidence section).

**Examination performance:** the Examination Application Map (§6) and Grammar School Readiness Definition (§11) directly target the actual mechanism of exam success — practising the right domains, to evidenced mastery, in the format the real exam uses — rather than assuming more practice of any kind is equally valuable.

**Long-term learning:** the Learning Transfer Map (§5) and Skill Dependencies (§3) operationalise AEP-001 §2.12's constitutional mandate that transfer be explicitly taught, not left to chance — knowledge built once and reinforced across contexts is retained and applicable long after a single exam, which is the entire premise of AEP-001 §2.2 (spacing) and §2.3 (interleaving).

### 2.3 Non-Verbal Reasoning — new competency taxonomy (this document)

`data/non-verbal-reasoning/*.ts` has real content (40 questions) but, like Verbal Reasoning before Decision 13, its `skill` field is uniformly `"non-verbal-reasoning"` — the real differentiator is its `category` field (12 raw values found by direct inspection). Consolidated into competency codes the same way §3 of `QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` did for Verbal Reasoning:

| Competency code | Consolidates raw `category` value(s) | Real question count | What it tests | Common student error (public-research-grounded) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nvr.pattern-completion` | Pattern Grids, Pattern Rules, Pattern Sequences | 11 | Inferring the rule governing a grid or sequence of shapes/symbols and applying it to find a missing element | Assuming the simplest visible pattern (e.g. a repeat) when the real rule is compound or two-dimensional |
| `nvr.symbol-codes` | Symbol Codes, Symbol Sequences, Number Grids | 8 | Applying a stated or inferred symbol-to-value mapping | Treating visually similar symbols as equivalent, or missing that a code applies positionally rather than absolutely |
| `nvr.rotation` | Rotation | 7 | Determining the result of rotating a shape or figure by a stated angle/direction | Confusing clockwise and anticlockwise, or applying the rotation around the wrong pivot point |
| `nvr.reflection-symmetry` | Reflection, Symmetry | 8 | Determining the result of reflecting a figure, or identifying/completing a symmetric figure | Reflecting along the wrong axis, or treating near-symmetry as exact symmetry |
| `nvr.shape-properties` | Shape Properties, Shape Counting | 5 | Identifying shared or differing properties across a set of figures (sides, angles, fills) | Counting a property inconsistently across figures presented at different sizes/orientations |
| `nvr.3d-shapes` | Nets and 3D Shapes | 1 | Relating a 2D net to the 3D shape it folds into | Misjudging which faces become adjacent once folded |

**Why the taxonomy stops at 6 codes, not 12:** the same "one primary competency per question, consolidate surface labels that test the same underlying skill" discipline established for Verbal Reasoning (Decision 13) and English (§2 of `ENGLISH_COMPETENCY_FRAMEWORK.md`) applies here — Reflection and Symmetry are genuinely the same underlying mirror-transformation reasoning tested two ways, so they are consolidated rather than kept as artificially separate competencies.

**Honest gap:** unlike Verbal Reasoning and Mathematics, this taxonomy has not yet been through a difficulty-calibration pass (`QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` §4-style), a worked-examples review, or real mastery-threshold tuning — it is a first-pass taxonomy derived directly from real content, offered here to complete the knowledge architecture, not a claim that Non-Verbal Reasoning is ready for ALI coverage. A dedicated extension of `QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` (a new §12, mirroring §3/§11's structure) is the natural next step, out of scope for this document.

### 2.4 Spatial Reasoning — new competency taxonomy (this document)

`data/spatial-reasoning/*.ts` — 39 real questions (category-count totals below are approximate; some questions carry secondary/overlapping category tags in the source data), same `skill: "spatial-reasoning"` uniform-value pattern:

| Competency code | Consolidates raw `category` value(s) | What it tests | Common student error |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sr.paper-folding` | Paper Folding | Predicting the result of one or more folds (layers, hole-punch outcomes) | Forgetting that each fold multiplies existing layers rather than adding to them |
| `sr.compass-grid-navigation` | Compass Directions, Grid Navigation | Tracking direction/position through a sequence of turns or grid moves | Confusing clockwise/anticlockwise turns, the same error family as `nvr.rotation` (see §3 — this is a real cross-domain link, not a coincidence) |
| `sr.3d-visualisation` | 3D Shapes, 3D Visualisation, Nets and 3D Shapes | Mentally rotating or assembling a 3D figure from a 2D representation | Judging a 3D shape from its 2D silhouette without accounting for the hidden/rotated faces |
| `sr.rotation` | Rotation | Determining the result of rotating a 3D or perspective figure | Same underlying error family as `nvr.rotation` — see §3 |
| `sr.shape-properties-symmetry` | Shape Properties, Symmetry | Identifying spatial properties (faces, symmetry) of solid or 2D figures | Treating an asymmetric view as symmetric due to a misleading 2D projection |

**Note on public evidence:** independent published guidance on Non-Verbal/Spatial Reasoning preparation converges on a specific, actionable finding worth carrying into AEP-003: students are rarely weak across the board — weakness typically concentrates in one or two specific pattern families, and once a family is explicitly taught, scores in that family improve sharply. This directly supports treating `nvr`/`sr` competencies as genuinely separate, individually-trackable skills (as this taxonomy now does) rather than one undifferentiated "reasoning ability."

`data/numerical-reasoning/*.ts` — 41 real questions, exact category-to-count reconciliation (unlike NVR/SR, this one balances exactly against the real question count):

| Competency code | Consolidates raw `category` value(s) | Real question count | What it tests | Common student error |

## 3. Skill Dependencies

Distinguishing, per `ALI_CROSS_SUBJECT_INTELLIGENCE.md` §1.1, **shared-mechanism** (evidence in one is real, if indirect, evidence about the other) from **sequential-dependency** (weakness upstream predicts but doesn't fully explain weakness downstream; strength upstream doesn't automatically transfer):

| Dependency | Type | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Place value → `maths.decimals`, `maths.percentages` | Sequential-dependency | NCETM research: children who haven't secured that each column right is 10× smaller (not just that each column left is 10× bigger) carry that gap directly into decimal and percentage errors |
| `maths.addition-subtraction` → `maths.multiplication`, `maths.division` | Sequential-dependency | Standard arithmetic-fluency progression; multiplication/division fluency is built on secure addition/subtraction, not an independent skill |
| `maths.fractions` → `maths.percentages`, `maths.ratio-proportion`, `numreason.percentages`, `numreason.ratio-proportion` | Shared-mechanism | The AEP-001 §2.12-mandated transfer chain — one proportional-reasoning structure recurring across four competency codes in two domains |
| Vocabulary → `vr.synonyms`, `vr.antonyms` | Sequential-dependency | Existing finding, `ALI_CROSS_SUBJECT_INTELLIGENCE.md` §1.2 |
| Reading Comprehension → Writing | Sequential-dependency | Existing finding, same source |
| `maths.algebra`, `maths.powers-roots` ↔ `vr.letter-codes`, `vr.number-codes` | Shared-mechanism (new finding) | Both require applying a stated symbolic rule consistently across multiple positions/steps — the same rule-application mechanism in a numeric vs. alphabetic domain |
| `nvr.pattern-completion` ↔ `vr.sequences` | Shared-mechanism (new finding) | Both require inferring a governing rule from a limited set of examples and extending it — visual vs. verbal domain, same underlying inference structure |
| `nvr.rotation` ↔ `sr.rotation`/`sr.compass-grid-navigation` | Shared-mechanism (new finding) | Both draw on the identical clockwise/anticlockwise mental-rotation skill — the common-error research in §2.4 shows this failure mode is the *same* error appearing in both domains, strong evidence for a real shared mechanism rather than a superficial resemblance |
| `maths.percentages`/`maths.ratio-proportion` ↔ `numreason.percentages`/`numreason.ratio-proportion` | Shared-mechanism (new finding) | Same competency, different question format — see §2.5 |

## 4. Common Misconceptions

Verbal Reasoning already carries a per-competency "common student error" column (`QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` §3) — not reproduced here. Extending the same discipline to every other domain, grounded rather than invented:

- **Mathematics** (NCETM-grounded, Strong evidence): fractions are frequently understood as "two separate whole numbers" rather than one value whose meaning depends on the numerator/denominator/whole relationship; place-value regrouping errors persist in addition/subtraction; decimal misconceptions specifically include treating the decimal point itself as "the fraction," and the asymmetric gap where children learn "each column left is 10× bigger" without the symmetric "each column right is 10× smaller."
- **English/Reading Comprehension** (Moderate evidence — derived from this project's own taxonomy work, not yet externally validated): conflating retrieval (what the text states) with inference (what the text implies) — `ENGLISH_COMPETENCY_FRAMEWORK.md` §2's own finding, that four differently-labelled question types all reduced to one inference skill, is itself indirect evidence that this distinction is exactly where confusion concentrates, since even question *authors* had blurred it under separate topic labels.
- **Vocabulary** (Moderate evidence, consistent with VR's own `vr.synonyms`/`vr.antonyms` error entry): treating phonetic or surface similarity as evidence of semantic similarity — a "sounds right" heuristic standing in for genuine meaning comparison.
- **Non-Verbal/Spatial Reasoning** (Strong evidence, public-research-grounded, §2.4): the *belief* that NVR/SR ability is fixed and unteachable is itself the most consequential misconception in this domain — not a content error but a mindset one, directly counter to the evidence that these skills are highly teachable once broken into discrete pattern families (§2.3–2.4).
- **A documentation-level misconception worth naming, not just a child's:** conflating Angel's `numreason` content subject with any exam board's own "Numerical Reasoning" paper label (§1) — this is a risk for parents and future contributors, not children, but belongs in this section because it could directly mislead a family about what has actually been practised.

**A structural finding worth carrying into AEP-004:** CEM's format — mixed, unpredictable question-type ordering under section time pressure — and ISEB's within-session adaptivity are both *format-fluency* requirements, not just content-coverage requirements. Angel's current mock structure (fixed sections per subject, `MOCK_CONFIGS`) trains content but does not train either of these formats specifically. This is named as a gap for AEP-004 (Learning Journey) to resolve, not something this document can resolve on its own.

**A second structural finding:** ALI's adaptive engine already changes difficulty *between* mocks based on accumulated evidence — a genuinely different mechanism from ISEB's *within-session* adaptivity, but a partial conceptual alignment worth noting rather than claiming as a full match.

**What is public record versus pending migration:** the paper structure, timing, and domain composition above are drawn from public sources cited inline and are treated as **Strong** evidence for structural claims. Anything requiring literal past-paper content — the actual proportion of marks a board allocates to a given competency, or genuine item-level difficulty benchmarking — is marked here as **Repository evidence pending migration from programme working papers**, not asserted.

## 7. Confidence Indicators

`LEARNING_PROFILE_MODEL.md` already defines and ships 7 candidate dimensions (Logical Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning, Numerical Confidence, Consistency, Learning Speed, Resilience, Confidence-vs-Accuracy), of which 3 are computable today from real bridged data and 4 are honestly typed `null` because the underlying per-attempt timestamp/response-time data doesn't exist yet — cited directly, not re-derived here.

This document adds one further indicator, at the competency-graph level rather than the whole-student level: a **per-competency confidence band**, derived from `mastery_state` combined with sessions-to-mastery evidence (AEP-001 §2.5's feedback-specificity evidence directly supports naming confidence at this granularity rather than only at the subject level).

**A constitutional constraint on how confidence indicators may ever be shown, not just what they measure:** per AEP-001 §2.9/§2.10 and `ANGEL_EXPERIENCE_MANIFESTO.md`'s Invisible Intelligence doctrine, a confidence indicator must never be surfaced to a child in a form that itself becomes a source of anxiety (a visible "weak" badge, a red flag on a subject). Confidence indicators are architecture for the system's own reasoning and for parent-facing plain-language summaries — never raw technical display to the child.

## 8. Typical Learning Progression

A genuine distinction, stated honestly rather than glossed over: Mathematics and English progression are **National Curriculum-referenced facts** (Strong evidence); Verbal/Non-Verbal/Spatial/Numerical Reasoning progression is **practitioner convention**, since these reasoning-paper skills are not part of the National Curriculum at all and are not formally taught in most primary schools (Moderate evidence — widely converged-upon guidance, not a curriculum document).

| Domain | Year 3–4 | Year 5–6 |
|---|---|---|
| Reading Comprehension | Infer characters' feelings/thoughts/motives from actions; predict what might happen next. [Source: National Curriculum English programme of study] | Same core inference skill, now explicitly required to be **justified with evidence from the text** — the justification requirement, not the inference skill itself, is what's new. [Source: same] |
| Mathematics | Foundational fluency (addition/subtraction/multiplication/division, simple fractions) | Multi-step reasoning, more complex fractions/decimals/percentages, algebraic thinking — matches `QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` §11's real content, which is entirely Year 5/6-pitched |
| Verbal/Non-Verbal/Spatial/Numerical Reasoning | Not formally taught in most schools; independent 11+ preparation guidance converges on beginning pattern-family exposure around Year 4 | Timed, exam-format practice concentrated in Year 5–6, with the widely-cited practitioner convention of starting serious preparation 12–18 months before the test date — **this is a preparation-industry convention, not a curriculum fact, and is tagged Moderate accordingly** |

## 9. Assessment Evidence

Angel's existing mastery model is the correct foundation and is not redesigned here: mastery requires correct answers across a `mastery_threshold` number of **distinct sessions** (not one lucky answer), and one incorrect attempt after mastery demotes the question back to `learning` (`ADAPTIVE_ASSESSMENT_ENGINE_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`, `QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` §8). This is directly grounded in AEP-001 §2.1 (retrieval practice/testing effect) — genuine evidence of learning is unaided recall demonstrated repeatedly under realistic conditions, not a single correct guess.

This document extends the standard from per-question to per-domain and whole-child evidence:

- **Domain-level evidence** of readiness requires sustained mastery across a *representative sample* of that domain's competencies, not just one or two — a child who has mastered `maths.addition-subtraction` alone has not demonstrated Mathematics readiness, regardless of how many questions they've answered correctly within that one competency.
- **Whole-child evidence** of exam-specific readiness requires evidence gathered under conditions that increasingly resemble the real exam — informal practice accuracy is necessary but not sufficient; genuine readiness evidence includes performance under timed, full-paper mock conditions (the natural handoff point to AEP-005, Assessment Framework).

## 11. Grammar School Readiness Definition

**This is the section that answers what this document exists to answer.** "Grammar School Ready" is not "has studied the topics on the syllabus." It is evidenced across five dimensions, and it is **pathway-relative** — a CSSE-ready child, a GL-ready child, a CEM-ready child, and an ISEB-ready child are not the same thing, because the exams genuinely test different domains and formats (§6).

1. **Content Coverage** — the child has practised every domain their target board(s) actually require (§6), and *only* those domains where practice time is scarce and finite. A CSSE-only family whose child spends significant time on Verbal or Non-Verbal Reasoning has not gained partial CSSE readiness from that time — CSSE tests neither — they have spent time that could have gone to English or Mathematics, the only two domains CSSE actually examines. "More domains practised" is not more readiness; it is only readiness if the domain is one the target board tests.

2. **Competency Mastery** — genuine, evidenced mastery (§9) across a representative sample of competencies within each required domain, not mere exposure or attempt volume. A child who has attempted every Mathematics competency but mastered none has not met this bar; a child who has mastered a representative sample across the domain has.

3. **Examination Fluency** — familiarity with the *specific* board's real format, timing, and pressure conditions (§6), not just its content: a GL-ready child has practised four separate, discrete timed papers; a CEM-ready child has practised mixed, unpredictable, section-timed online format under CEM's specific pressure profile; an ISEB-ready child has experienced within-session difficulty adaptation. Content mastery without format fluency is incomplete readiness for that specific board.

4. **Transfer & Resilience** — the child can apply mastered competencies to unfamiliar phrasing and contexts (§5), not just to questions matching exactly what was practised, and recovers from an error or a bad practice session without a single mastery regression becoming a confidence collapse (AEP-001 §2.9).

5. **Confidence & Wellbeing** — the child arrives at the real exam "capable, confident, and relatively calm" (AEP-001 §2.9, §2.10). A child who is content-ready, competency-mastered, and format-fluent, but anxious, has **not** met this constitution's definition of Grammar School Ready — this dimension is not a nice-to-have alongside the other four, it is co-equal and non-negotiable per AEP-001's Educational Safety Principle.

**Grammar School Ready, stated as a single sentence (original five dimensions):** a child is Grammar School Ready for a specific pathway when they have practised exactly the domains that pathway's real exam requires, to evidenced and representative competency mastery, in a format resembling that exam's actual conditions, with demonstrated ability to transfer that mastery to unfamiliar material and recover from setbacks, while remaining capable, confident, and calm about doing so. **This sentence is now read together with §11.6 below, added by APD-003 — it is not rewritten, since the amendment adds a sixth dimension rather than altering the first five.**

### 11.6 Learning Independence (Amendment — APD-003)

A sixth, co-equal dimension of Grammar School Readiness: the learner should **progressively** demonstrate the ability to plan their own learning, identify their own weaknesses, review their own mistakes, revise independently, and reflect on their own progress. Angel should gradually reduce the guidance it provides as learner maturity increases, rather than either withholding independence-building entirely or assuming it prematurely.

**This is deliberately constrained by AEP-001 §2.8 (Metacognition and Self-Regulated Learning), not a departure from it.** §2.8 established that metacognitive capacity develops through middle childhood and is not reliably independent in most 8–11-year-olds — an 8-year-old cannot be expected to self-regulate the way a 16-year-old might. Learning Independence as a readiness dimension is therefore **progressive and scaffolded by design**: what Angel expects and measures for a Year 3–4 learner (mostly system-managed, per §2.8's scaffolding recommendation) is not what it expects for a Year 6 learner approaching the exam (more self-directed review and reflection, still supported, never assumed fully autonomous). A learner does not "fail" this dimension for needing scaffolding appropriate to their age — they fail it only if the *trajectory* toward greater independence isn't present as they mature, or if Angel's own design never reduces guidance at all regardless of demonstrated maturity.

**Why this improves a child's chances of success:** Learning Independence is not only a readiness signal for the exam itself — it is what AEP-001 §2.7 (self-determination theory) identifies as the autonomy component of sustained intrinsic motivation, and what §2.9's long-runway spacing requirement depends on: a child who can identify their own weak spots and choose to revisit them is better positioned to sustain the months-long preparation timeline than one who is entirely dependent on the system to decide everything for them.

## 13. Pathway-First Architecture (Amendment — APD-003)

Angel is a multi-pathway learning platform. Every learner selects a pathway during onboarding, and the selected pathway governs curriculum, learning journey, recommendations, assessments, analytics, and the readiness model — not as six separate pathway-aware features, but as one governing input read consistently by all six. Supported pathways, extending §1/§6's four-board analysis:

| Pathway | Status in this document |
|---|---|
| CSSE | Fully mapped, §6 |
| GL Assessment | Fully mapped, §6 |
| CEM | Fully mapped, §6 |
| ISEB | Fully mapped, §6 |
| Independent School | **New in this amendment — not yet mapped.** Structurally different from the other four: independent schools set their own bespoke entrance assessments rather than sharing one of the four standardised boards, so there is no single "Independent School format" to document the way §6 documents CSSE/GL/CEM/ISEB. This pathway needs its own discovery treatment (likely per-school or per-cluster, not a single format table) before an Examination Application Map entry is possible — flagged as an open item, not fabricated here. |
| Custom Programme | **New in this amendment — not yet mapped.** Unlike the other five, this pathway is not tied to any specific exam board at all (e.g. general enrichment, or a family without a fixed target school) — its "requirements" are set by the family/curriculum choice rather than a board's format, so §6's board-mapping approach does not directly apply. Needs its own definition of what governs curriculum/journey/readiness for this pathway before it can be treated as fully specified. |

**Why this is architectural, not just organisational:** per §11's Grammar School Readiness Definition, Content Coverage is explicitly pathway-relative — practising a domain the learner's pathway doesn't test is not neutral, it is an opportunity cost (Real Gaps #5). Pathway-First Architecture is the constitutional statement that this relativity must govern every downstream decision (what content is recommended, how the journey sequences, what a mock exam looks like, what analytics are shown, what "ready" means for this specific learner) consistently, not just in the Readiness Definition alone.

## 14. Terminology Governance (Amendment — APD-003)

Educational terminology shown to families must always match the selected examination pathway's own vocabulary, and internal implementation names must never leak into or influence public-facing educational terminology. This directly resolves the naming-collision finding already raised in §1 and Real Gaps #4:

| Term | Governed meaning | Where it may be used |
|---|---|---|
| **Mathematics** | The curriculum-based numerate skill domain — Angel's `maths.*` competency codes (arithmetic, algebra, geometry, fractions, etc., `QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` §11) | Any pathway; this is the standard, unambiguous term |
| **Mathematical Reasoning** | Applying mathematical knowledge to solve non-standard, puzzle-style reasoning problems — the recommended **public-facing** relabelling of Angel's internal `numreason` content subject (§2.5) | Any pathway, as Angel's own generic content-subject name — deliberately *not* called "Numerical Reasoning" publicly, to avoid the collision below |
| **Numerical Reasoning** | A specific exam board's own paper name for a *combined* Mathematics + Non-Verbal Reasoning construct — currently only CEM uses this exact label this way (§6) | **Only** when referring to that specific board's paper by name (e.g. "CEM's Numerical Reasoning paper") — never as Angel's own generic subject label |
| **Verbal Reasoning** | Word/language-based reasoning — Angel's `vr.*` competency codes | Standard term across GL/CEM/ISEB; no collision, safe to use generically |
| **Non-Verbal Reasoning** | Visual/pattern-based reasoning — Angel's `nvr.*` competency codes | Standard term across GL/CEM/ISEB; no collision, safe to use generically |

**The governing rule, stated plainly:** an internal engineering identifier (a database subject key, a folder name, a variable name) is never itself evidence of what a family should be told — `numreason` is a legitimate internal name for a codebase; "Mathematical Reasoning," not "Numerical Reasoning," is what a parent or child should ever see it called, precisely because "Numerical Reasoning" already means something different and board-specific (CEM's combined paper) that Angel's internal subject does not fully match.

## Real Gaps Found

Stated plainly, not smoothed over:

1. **Non-Verbal Reasoning, Spatial Reasoning, and Numerical Reasoning had no competency taxonomy at all before this document**, despite having 119 real questions combined — a genuine, previously invisible gap. This document creates a first-pass taxonomy but does not attempt the difficulty-calibration or worked-example depth `QUESTION_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` §3/§11 provide for VR/Maths; a dedicated extension of that standard is the natural next step.
2. **No probability competency exists anywhere in Angel's content**, in any domain — surfaced directly by the AEP-001 §2.12-mandated transfer chain naming it explicitly. **Formally recorded as GAP-001 in the new `CURRICULUM_GAP_REGISTER.md` (APD-003)** — no content is authored here; the register exists to track this and future gaps for prioritised future educational releases, not to resolve them now.
3. **Writing has no competency model or gradable mechanic at all** — reconfirms `ENGLISH_COMPETENCY_FRAMEWORK.md` §5's existing finding; this document did not find a way around it.
4. **A real naming collision**: Angel's `numreason` content subject and CEM's "Numerical Reasoning" exam paper are not the same thing, and nothing in the product currently prevents a parent from conflating them (§1, §6).
5. **CSSE-pathway families are structurally exposed to a real opportunity-cost risk**: Angel's current content/mock structure does not distinguish pathway-relevant domains from irrelevant ones, meaning a CSSE-only child could spend meaningful practice time on Verbal/Non-Verbal/Spatial Reasoning that their actual target exam does not test at all. This is named here as a finding for **AEP-004 (Learning Journey Framework)** to resolve — it is a sequencing/targeting problem, not a knowledge-architecture gap this document can close on its own.
6. **CEM's mixed/unpredictable format and ISEB's within-session adaptivity are format-fluency requirements Angel's current mock structure does not train** — named for AEP-004.
